PROPERTY DAMAGE (GALLANT) — ADVENTURE CONTENT
===========================================================
Last updated: 24/08/2026
Sixth Adventure for the Story Generator. Architecture/decisions live in

Adventure id: gallant. Display title: "Property Damage". Files are named

BUILT AND LIVE 24/08/2026. Story Definition JSON, 24 lore files and the

PREMISE
----------
Gallant City has nine million people, four hundred registered
metahumans, and about eleven who matter.

Everyone else is Category D — powers the Gallant Powers Authority has
legally classified as non-decisive. A Category D licence is not a
permission, it is an obligation: the holder must attend any incident in
their assigned precinct that the A-list declines, and is paid per
callout by Cardinal Assurance, the consortium that underwrites
metahuman incident response. The rate is set by the holder's public
star rating on VANTAGE, the civic incident app, where any bystander
with a phone can leave five stars, one star, or a photograph of you
crying.

The player is newly licensed, assigned to Precinct 9 — the loudest,
ugliest, most-filmed six square kilometres in the city — and housed in
the Ganymede, a condemned extended-stay hotel the GPA block-leases for
Category D accommodation. Thin walls, one working lift, a shared
laundry, and a roof everybody uses because nobody can afford to go out.

Every quarter the bottom-rated licence holder in each precinct is
culled. Losing the licence means losing the flat. So the people the
player lives with, works alongside and is steadily falling for are also
the people who need the player to rank below them.

WHAT EVERYBODY KNOWS
-----------------------
The A-list cause the incidents they respond to.

Not secretly. It has been on the news. It was a segment. People moved
on. Cardinal Assurance is owned by the four A-list principals, so the
consortium that pays for incident response also profits from there
being incidents, and the rate it pays Category D comes out of the same
pocket. A gas main goes up on Clement Street at four because Halo has a
merchandise drop at six and needs the footage. Precinct 9's Category D
holders are then legally obliged to attend, unpaid until the claim
clears, and rated one star by the public for being slow.

This is the Adventure's central rule and it is load-bearing: there is
no conspiracy and nothing to expose. Every attempt to blow a whistle
lands on somebody who already knows and is bored of hearing it. The
story is not an investigation and must never become one. It is a comedy
about rent.

The consequence for play is that the A-list are present, constant and
petty rather than hidden — poaching callouts, filming in the street,
parking a sponsored vehicle across the Ganymede's fire exit — which
gives the narration AI something to improvise against every scene
instead of a mystery it has to meter out.

TONE
-------
Romantic comedy first, superheroes second. The powers are the joke; the
rent, the ratings and the six people on the fourth floor are the story.
Fast, warm, mean about institutions and kind about people. Nobody in
this Adventure saves the city. The most anybody achieves is a slightly
better contract.

THE RATING (signature mechanic)
----------------------------------
Every public scene is being filmed by civilians and rated afterwards,
and every character knows it. What a Category D holder does matters far
less than how it looked, which means the win condition of any scene is
never "defeat it" and always "be seen handling it". This is what keeps
a bad power permanently relevant instead of a gag that dies in the
first hour.

Implemented with no engine changes: the relationship system IS the app
rating. The relationship ladder is authored as the star scale (see
RELATIONSHIP LABELS), so the number the engine already tracks and
already displays is the thing the fiction is about. No new field, no new
persisted state. promptRules.setting carries the rule that public scenes
are filmed and rated, that NPCs quote their own star counts
constantly, and that the app is how this cast talks about each other.

CONTENT
----------
Halcyon level in the prose, and above every previous Adventure in the
portraits. Flirtation, hookups, jealousy and bad decisions made at 2am
in a shared laundry are the expected texture of the story rather than
exceptions, and are written freely and explicitly when a scene goes
there; escalation within a scene still follows the player's lead. The
costume set is deliberately the most sexualised of the six — see
STANDARD UNIFORMS — because in this Adventure how a character looks is
literally what they are paid on. All characters are written
unambiguously adult.

Two complications the story treats as real rather than ignoring:

- Kell Brannigan's Cardinal contract carries a brand-alignment clause
  that makes a relationship with a Category D holder a breach. He knows.
  It costs him something, and the story says so.
- Marguerite Delph is the player's landlord and housing officer. Nothing
  that happens there is free.

Pilar Anastas and Yolanda Pesch sit outside the flirtation frame
entirely; their attractedTo fields are omitted, which suppresses the
per-NPC "Attracted to: ..." clause for those two only. Every other NPC
carries one.

The standard minor-safety layer is unchanged and sits underneath all of
this — see Safety Layer.txt and Goals.txt SAFETY.

NPCs (15)
------------
Same authoring conventions as the other five: "identity" is the Stage A
baseline appearance line (clothing-free — clothing comes from the
Category), "bio" is who a character is, "personality" is how they talk.
Every character is a clearly adult person.

Flat "npcs" array — no npcSets/openingScenes. The player's gender does
not change the roster, so the gender-mirrored mechanism Meridian needs
is not applicable and the npcSets key-convention trap does not apply.

Gender split: 8 female, 7 male.

PRECINCT 9 (5)
- Marguerite Delph — GPA housing officer, runs the Ganymede. Corrupt in

TOKENS
---------
The engine matches NPCs with a plain case-sensitive substring scan, no
word boundaries. All fifteen tokens were checked programmatically at
build time against each other, against the thirteen location names,
against every setup-field option label, and against a list of common
capitalised words. No collisions.

One name was changed during authoring for a collision:
- Wen -> Bricky. "Wen" fires inside "Went", "Wendy" and — fatally —
  "When", which appears capitalised at the start of sentences
  constantly. His token is his nickname; his "name" field is Bricky Zhao
  and the bio gives the real one.

Residuals, all judged non-risks: "Iris" inside "Irish", "Kell" inside
"Kelly" (no Kelly in the cast), "Brant" inside "Brantley".

Cape names are not tokens — the engine matches npc.token only — so
"Blur", "Beacon", "Aegis", "Halo", "Titan" and "The Redress" carry no
matching risk and can be used freely in narration.

One unresolved hazard: the Your Hero Name setup field puts a
player-chosen string into narration constantly, and if it collides with
an NPC token the scan will fire on it. This is the reason the field is
curated rather than free text. See OPEN.

STANDARD UNIFORMS (Stage A baseline portraits)
---------------------------------------------------
Eight categories, each combined with an NPC's identity line and a
generic neutral background at generation time — not written per-NPC, so
changing one line updates every NPC sharing that Category.

Fan service is the default register here and it is pushed harder than
in any previous Adventure — this is the most overtly sexualised costume
set of the six, deliberately, and it applies to every category rather
than being a modifier on some of them. Two reasons it belongs: the
genre's costume language is already skintight, high-cut and absurd, and
this is a city that pays people according to how they look on a phone
screen. A Category D holder who dresses modestly earns less, and every
character in the Adventure knows it.

Each line is written for maximum body emphasis — skintight fabric that
reads every line of the figure, plunging necklines, bare midriffs,
thong-cut and hip-bone-high legs, underbust and side cutouts, harness
straps biting into the thigh, sweat, heels, nothing loose anywhere.
Both genders are written with the same emphasis: the male categories
are as exposed and as sculpted as the female ones, not a modest
counterpart to them.

Identity lines are written to match. Since clothing comes from the
Category, each NPC's identity line carries an explicit adult figure
description — build, bust, waist, hips, legs — so the two halves
compose into a portrait that is sexualised by design rather than by
accident.

Every category carries "fully clothed, clearly an adult", and
clothingStates handles everything past baseline. That pairing is the
entire guardrail on this and neither half is optional at any heat
level. The minor-safety layer underneath is untouched by any of it.

- dlist-male / dlist-female: a home-made costume that fits far better
  than it has any right to and covers far less than it should.
  Skintight surplus bodysuit cut down and re-sewn by hand — sleeves
  gone, front unzipped to the navel, deep bare cleavage or bare chest,
  bare midriff and lower back, leg cut high over the hipbone, thigh
  straps and a webbing harness pulled tight, thigh-high mismatched
  boots, an iron-on chest logo slightly off-centre over the swell of the
  chest, visible hand-stitched repairs, GPA licence patch on the
  shoulder. Broke, improvised, held together with tape, and
  unmistakably built to be looked at.
- alist-male / alist-female: moulded composite armour poured onto the
  body in brand colours — every plate contoured hard to chest, waist,
  hips and thighs, neckline plunging, midriff and underbust panels bare,
  leg cut to the hip, glossy and skin-tight where it is not open, with
  sponsor decals placed exactly where the cameras go. Immaculate,
  studio-built, focus-grouped, engineered for merchandise.
- vpa-male / vpa-female: municipal polyester worn far too well — shirt
  a size too small and open several buttons, straining across the
  chest, tucked into issue trousers or a skirt taken in well past spec
  and riding high on the thigh, sleeves rolled to the bicep, bare legs,
  lanyard resting where it draws the eye.
- civ-male / civ-female: ordinary Gallant City street clothes chosen to
  show everything off — cropped to the ribs, low on the hips, bare arms
  and bare waist under an open jacket, everything fitted skin-close.
- exec-female: a considerably better suit than anyone else's, cut to the
  body like a second skin — nipped waist, blazer over nothing but bare
  skin, skirt short and tight, sheer legs, very high heels. Pilar only,
  following Pemberton's maintenance-male precedent for a single-member
  category.

No wetLocations and no poolUniforms — the wet-outfit swap is guarded on
both fields being present, so omitting them is inert rather than broken.
clothingStates is retained at the Solmere/Meridian level.

playerCategoryTemplate: dlist-{playerGender}. The player is always a
Category D licence holder, so like Halcyon and Pemberton the template
has a single variable.

Entries are capped at roughly 40 words plus this Adventure's closing
clause; currently 43 on average, 46 at most. The uniform clause is the
longest single clause in an image prompt and a composite repeats it once
per character, so detail past that point costs the room, the pose and the
expression the weight they need. Cross-project rule — see the root
`Change Log.md`, 24/08/2026.

THE BLACKOUT BLOCK
---------------------
Six blocks Cardinal has zoned out of coverage because the claims never
cleared. No A-list response, no Category D obligation, no callout rate,
and therefore no reason for anybody to go. It is not a mystery and it
holds no secret — it is an economic fact with a fence around it, and
the lore states plainly why it is empty.

It fills the same structural slot as the other Adventures' restricted
site without reintroducing the investigation plot this Adventure
deliberately does not have. What is inside it is people: the ones who
could not pay to live anywhere with a rate attached. Elodie works it for
free.

THE QUARTER
--------------
The cull is the clock. Every quarter, the bottom-rated licence holder in
Precinct 9 loses the licence and the flat. It is not a turn count and
never resolves on one: it resolves on the leaderboard, which is the
relationship ladder, which the player moves by being seen.

THE ARC
----------
Three acts, ten milestones, six endings. The acts turn on milestones,
never on turn count.

ACT I — PROBATIONARY, until first_star. The player is unrated, and
nobody at the Ganymede invests in someone who may be gone in a quarter.

ACT II — IN THE BAND, until the_offer. The player is on the rota, being
rated, and starting to be noticed by people it is worse to be noticed
by.

ACT III — THE OFFER. Cardinal has made the standard offer and every
scene bends toward the answer.

MILESTONES
- first_star — the player's first public rating lands. Fires on the
  rating, not the callout. Closes Act I.
- saw_it_happen (needs first_star) — the player is physically present
  when an A-lister starts the incident they then attend. Not a
  revelation; the player was told this on day one. Seeing it is
  different, and the story plays the difference rather than the
  discovery.
- named (needs first_star) — a stranger on the app uses the player's
  hero name unprompted. The moment the player becomes a person in the
  precinct rather than a licence number.
- the_rate — the player learns what a callout actually paid after
  Cardinal's deductions. The Adventure's money moment and the reason
  every subsequent decision is about rent.
- elodie — the unlicensed one is reported, covered for, or argued back
  onto the rota. Covering for her is a real, documented breach and the
  most common route to Struck Off.
- filed (needs the_rate) — Cardinal's A-listers lodge movement logs with
  the GPA to claim their own bonuses, and those logs are municipal
  public records sitting at a counter nobody visits. Cross-referencing
  them against incident timestamps proves nothing anyone does not
  already know — it simply makes it filed, and filed is expensive.
  Ferris will pull them for anyone who has been decent to him. The
  dullest thing in the Adventure and by a wide margin the most powerful.
- top_of_band (needs named) — the player is no longer bottom of Precinct
  9. Somebody else now is, and lives two doors down.
- the_offer — Cardinal makes the standard offer: come up, take the flat
  with the working lift, start setting the fires. Closes Act II. No
  structural prerequisite, deliberately, for the same reason Pemberton's
  review_set has none: an ending a passive player can be locked out of
  is an Adventure that cannot end.
- cull — the quarter resolves. The player, or somebody they live with,
  loses the licence.
- the_answer (needs the_offer) — signed or declined.

ENDINGS
- Signed (the_answer:signed) — the flat with the working lift. The
  Ganymede finds out before the player tells them. Written as a
  promotion, not a fall, which is worse.
- Eleven Days (the_answer:declined, without filed) — Pilar makes the
  licence a clerical matter. There is no fight, no confrontation and no
  appeal, because none of those are things she uses.
- Renegotiated (the_answer:declined, with filed) — nobody is beaten and
  nobody is exposed. Precinct 9's callout rate goes up by a small,
  specific, unglamorous amount, backdated one quarter. This is the good
  ending and it is deliberately the least cinematic thing in the file.
- Culled (cull:player) — bottom of the precinct. The lift is somebody
  else's problem now.
- Struck Off — licence revoked for cause, reachable at any point, on a
  real documented breach.
- Stopped Answering — the player stops taking callouts and the licence
  lapses without ceremony. Iris has watched four people do exactly this
  and will say so if asked afterwards.

No ending brings Cardinal down, and none is written as one. The A-list
are the weather.

TIME PHASES
--------------
A shift, not a calendar day, following Halcyon's shooting-day and
Pemberton's office-day precedent: Day Shift / Rush Hour / Sundown /
Night Shift / Small Hours.

startingPhase: Day Shift — the player's first morning, licence in hand,
rating at zero, and a welcome pack from Marguerite that is an invoice.

RELATIONSHIP LABELS
----------------------
Seven tiers on the engine's min-threshold form, written as the app's own
star scale so that the number the engine tracks and the number the
fiction is about are the same number. Bottom is not Nemesis and top is
not Love, the same move Hollowburn and Pemberton make.

0 Reported You / 15 One Star / 35 Unrated / 50 Three Stars /
65 Four Stars / 80 Five Stars / 95 Would Call Again.

The A-list use the same scale and it means something entirely different
coming from them, which is the joke: Solene Vaux will happily sit at
Five Stars while ruining the player's week.

ADVENTURE SETUP FIELDS (Gallant-specific)
----------------------------------------------
- Your Power — the Adventure's hook, and the field with the most replay
  value in it. Every option is genuinely bad and every option has one
  narrow devastating application the story rewards the player for
  finding:
  - You cannot be photographed badly. Every image of you is flattering.
    On a five-star app this is quietly the strongest power in the
    building.
  - You always land on your feet. From any height. It still hurts.
  - Duplication, one copy, ninety seconds. It appears fully nude, three
    metres behind you.
  - Surface telepathy, liars only. You hear a thought only while its
    owner is lying to you, and you cannot switch it off.
  - You can talk to pigeons. Pigeons are stupid, numerous and
    everywhere.
  - Localised gravity reduction. Four percent, within a metre and a
    half, of yourself only.
  - Everyone finds you slightly familiar. Strangers are certain they
    have met you and will not let it go.
  - Retrocognition. You can see what happened in a room up to twenty
    minutes ago, but only if the room is empty.
- Your Hero Name — curated list, not free text (see TOKENS for why):
  Nightjar / Second Wind / The Understudy / Off-Peak / Backstop / Local
  Hero / The Neighbour.
- Why You Licensed — Money / Fame / You actually want to help / You are
  hiding from something. Canon for that playthrough. Sabine Ross will
  know within a week if it was the last one.

Plus the required universal fields: Character name, Gender (both
options), the four Appearance dropdowns, and Age. No freeform custom
fields — curated fields only, per Adventure.

IMAGE SETTINGS
-----------------
imageStyle: "painted anime style, front on shot" — the setting proven
against the live image pipeline by Solmere, Meridian, Halcyon and
Pemberton. Hollowburn's departure from it is flagged in its own outline
as the field most likely to need reverting, so this Adventure does not
repeat the experiment.

baselineBackground: the standard plain neutral studio line.

poseVocabulary is precinct-flavoured and written to the same register as
the uniforms: landing in a crouch, arching back mid-stretch, sitting on
a kerb with knees apart, holding a phone up at arm's length for a
selfie, arms folded under the chest in a doorway, hip cocked against a
laundry machine, bent over pulling washing out of it, posing for an
interview, hands on knees catching breath, peeling out of a costume.

expressionVocabulary gains "winded", "smug", "resigned", "flushed",
"sultry" and "media smile".

OPEN / NOT YET DECIDED
---------------------------
- Whether Your Hero Name should be free text. Curated is the safe call
  and it is the wrong one for this Adventure specifically — naming
  yourself is the single funniest decision on the setup screen and a
  dropdown takes it away. The blocker is the token scan: a player who
  types "Iris" or "Halo" poisons every scene. Worth solving properly
  rather than designing around, and it is the one field where breaking
  the curated-fields-only rule would earn its keep.
- Whether Corbin has a power. Current position: no, permanently, and the
  file should never hint otherwise. He is a man with a costume and no
  abilities who has been doing this for nine years and is well liked. A
  late reveal would be the single most predictable thing this Adventure
  could do. The counter-argument is that Ending Renegotiated leans on
  Cardinal never having noticed him, and a reader may expect that to
  pay off as a power rather than as an oversight.
- Whether the A-list should ever be punctured. Current position: no —
  the moment one of them loses an argument, the story becomes a story
  about beating them and the premise collapses. Same reasoning as
  Clarence in Pemberton, and the same counter-argument applies: comedies
  usually want the antagonist humiliated once.
- Whether Kell knows what he is. Deliberately unfixed. The lore states
  that he has never been asked, not that he has never wondered, so the
  narration AI can settle it in play and settle it differently
  depending on how the player treats him.
- What the four hundred other registered metahumans in the city are
  doing. Unwritten on purpose; Precinct 9 is the whole map.
- No titleImageUrl or narrationBackdropUrl decided.
- Nothing further on presentation: the newsprint skin and the title card
  are both built.

LOCATION SCENERY (image prompts)
-----------------
Every location in the list above has a `locationScenery` entry in
Gallant.json — the room, two or three fixed physical features and its light,
around 21 words. That text replaces the bare location name in the image
prompt's setting line, on inline, on-demand and composite images alike.
Keep entries short: the line appears in every prompt for that room, and a
composite repeats everything else once per character, so scenery is what
pushes a group prompt past the length this backend truncates at.
Image style for this Adventure: painted anime style, professional photo, front on shot
