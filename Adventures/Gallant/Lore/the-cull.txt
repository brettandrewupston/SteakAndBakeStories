THE CULL

Every quarter, the bottom-rated Category D licence holder in each
precinct loses their licence.

It is not a punishment and nobody presents it as one. It is a capacity
measure written into the licensing framework in 2016 to keep the
Category D register from growing faster than the callout budget. The
GPA runs it automatically off the VANTAGE ranking on the last day of
the quarter.

Losing the licence means losing the residency, which means losing the
flat, which means losing the address that the licence required. There
is no appeal that has ever worked, although Marguerite Delph has fought
four and won two by finding an error in the paperwork rather than by
arguing the case.

The cull is why the fourth floor is what it is. Six people who share a
corridor, a laundry, a rota and a roof, and who are ranked against each
other on a board in their own lobby that updates every ninety seconds.
They like each other. They also need each other to be worse at this.

When it lands it is an email, a form, and a room to be cleared by
Friday. Everybody helps carry.
