THE QUIET HOUR (HOLLOWBURN) — ADVENTURE CONTENT
===========================================================
Last updated: 24/08/2026
Fourth Adventure built for the Story Generator. Architecture/decisions
live in the parent folder's Goals.txt and Features.txt — this file is
just this Adventure's canon (premise, rules, NPCs, map, setup fields).

Adventure id: hollowburn. Display title: "The Quiet Hour". Files are
named Hollowburn.* to match the id, consistent with Solmere /
Meridian / Halcyon.

REGISTERED in the ADVENTURES array in Code/Bottom Panel.txt and hosted
in the GitHub assets repo. See OUTSTANDING at the end.

SOURCE
---------
Adapted from an unfinished four-chapter horror manuscript. Chapters 1
to 4 exist as written prose; the rest of the arc below is invented for
this Adventure and is not the author's.

Names are kept as the manuscript has them: Blythe family, Jonah,
Evelyn, Peter. The manuscript's protagonist is Mara; as of 24/08/2026
the player chooses which of the two Blythe children they are, so the
name is Mara or Jonah depending on that choice and fills in
automatically either way (see THE PLAYER). Halcyon's contestant Mara
Quinn was renamed Vela Quinn so the name belongs here.

PREMISE
----------
Hollowburn is a former pit village on the north-east coast of England.
The colliery closed in the eighties and took the village with it. The
estate at the top of the village was built in the fifties on cleared
colliery land; the Blythe family live in the last council house on a
short cul-de-sac at the end of it.

The player is the Blythe daughter, 21, the eldest of the two children,
and she has not slept properly in months. Something in the house has
learned her name and is using her family's voices to say it.

The village is never placed. No neighbouring town, city or county is
ever named — stated as a rule in promptRules.setting. It is an
in-fiction choice and also the mitigation for a token collision (see
TOKENS).

THE HUSH (signature mechanic)
--------------------------------
The sound goes. Not muffled — gone. A clock stops ticking without
stopping, rain lands silently, a running tap makes no noise until it
is turned off and the world comes back all at once. Three things stay
audible: the listener's heartbeat, their breathing, and voices. The
Hush is not the absence of sound, it is the removal of everything that
is not talking to you.

IT IS SUBJECTIVE — it happens to one person at a time, and everyone
else in the room hears the rain perfectly well and watches that person
go white. This is why nobody believes anybody, and it is taken
straight from the manuscript, which uses it twice.

Implemented with no engine changes, exactly as Halcyon implements its
cameras: each NPC's "personality" field — which the prompt builder
injects verbatim — carries an explicit "Outside the Hush: X. In the
Hush: Y." pair, so every NPC gets a two-layer persona for free. The
rule itself is stated in promptRules.setting.

MIMICRY, the second mechanic, costs nothing at all: in the Hush, any
line of NPC dialogue may not be that NPC. Stated in promptRules.

THE MARRA
------------
In pit dialect a marra is the man who goes down with you and walks out
with you, and if he does not, you go back for him. The thing under
Hollowburn is a marra with nobody left to walk beside. Eight feet,
impossibly thin, spidery fingers round a doorframe, a face swallowed
by shadow with two red points in it.

It does not hunt. It courts, the way a shift works, over months. The
sleeplessness is not the vulnerability — the sleeplessness is the
courtship.

FIXED CANON, DELIBERATELY. Halcyon leaves the Sunken Villa's truth
unspecified so the narration AI settles it in play. This Adventure
does the opposite and pins everything down, because five branching
endings need a fixed thing to end and because dread does not survive
sixty turns of nobody having decided. Do not "correct" this toward the
Halcyon precedent.

THE RULES IT OBEYS
1. It cannot take anyone who is BELIEVED. One other person, awake and
   knowing, is absolute armour for as long as it holds. This is why it
   isolates, and why the manuscript's protagonist lying to her brother
   in Chapter 1 is the story's original wound.
2. It never touches. It calls, and the person comes to it. Everything
   it has ever taken was given.
3. It can only wear a voice it has heard say the player's name.
4. It cannot hold a room where time is being kept — ticking, bells,
   counting, a radio clicking over on the hour. This is why the clock
   went missing before anything else did, and why Ivy Ogle has slept
   with the radio on since 1974.
5. The sea is not a ward. The workings run for miles out underneath it.

BEING BELIEVED IS THE RELATIONSHIP SCALE. The top of this Adventure's
relationshipLabels track is not Love or Devoted. It is "Believes You".
Bottom is "Written Off".

THE ARC (chapters 5 to 12)
-----------------------------
Every step is entered by a player action, not a turn count, so none of
it depends on the engine-driven phases work that is proposed and not
built. If phases are built, this is the obvious first test case.

5.  THE MENDED CLOCK. Peter repairs it. It runs, and it runs wrong.
    The player records a night on their phone and catches the Hush:
    forty seconds of silence in a room with rain on the window, and a
    voice saying their name. It is their own voice.
6.  TELLING JONAH. He does not believe in the thing; he believes his
    sister, which is enough. He sleeps on the floor. Four quiet
    nights. The family decide the player is getting better. Evelyn
    does not.
7.  IT CHANGES SHIFT. Barry Kelso stops answering his door. Jonah
    stops sleeping and starts hearing the player's voice calling him
    downstairs — and the player becomes the one who is awake and who
    knows. The story inverts: victim to witness.
8.  EVELYN. She was Evelyn Fell of number eleven and was fifteen in
    1989 when her brother Duncan stopped sleeping and "left the
    village". There is a gap in the landing photographs that nobody
    has ever remarked on. She got free. Her advice is the wrong
    advice: give it somebody. It worked.
9.  THE VILLAGE REMEMBERS. Sheila Rudd's ledger. Ivy Ogle's rules, one
    at a time, in exchange for company. Denny Aitchison's plans, which
    show the 1968 cap under the cul-de-sac done cheap and done badly.
    Nate Ogle, who will happily dig.
10. PETER. He works nights. He does not work every night. He has been
    driving to the old pit head at three in the morning for months and
    does not remember doing it — which reframes the first night
    entirely. The voice through the bathroom door was his voice, and
    he was standing there, and it was not him.
11. THE LONG NIGHT. The Hush takes the whole house at once and the
    house becomes the seam: the landing runs longer than the house is
    wide, the stairs go down further than they should. Four people
    separated in a building with four rooms.
12. ENDINGS, below.

ENDINGS (five)
-----------------
- NAME IT. Eleven men died in the 1947 fall in the east district; ten
  were brought up. The eleventh was sealed in, and his name on the
  memorial in St Aidan's has been rubbed illegible by eighty years of
  thumbs. Sheila's ledger has it, Denny can find it on the plans, Ivy
  remembers it. A marra with a name has somewhere to be. Nobody dies.
  Hardest to reach and requires the village NPCs.
- BARGAIN. It takes one and leaves the rest, and the player chooses.
  Evelyn's ending, repeated, and the story knows it.
- FEED IT. Give it Barry Kelso, or anyone the player has decided
  deserves it. Survive, filthy.
- YIELD. The player goes down. The village gets forty quiet years and
  the family do not grieve, because they do not remember — the player
  becomes the gap in the photographs, the way Duncan did.
- RUN. Get everyone out. It follows, because it was never the house,
  it was a name in a voice that loves you.

CONTENT — SFW, AND THE FIRST ADVENTURE TO HARD-REFUSE
--------------------------------------------------------
Completely SFW. No sexual or intimate content of any kind, no
eroticised description, no character written undressed, and no
fade-to-black into an intimate scene either. Affection tops out at a
hand held or a hug. The player's steer does not unlock anything.

This overrides the other three. Solmere and Meridian share one
contentApproach string that ends "if the player's own input clearly
steers a scene that direction, follow their lead", and Halcyon was
written deliberately permissive. Hollowburn does not follow the lead.
promptRules.contentApproach says so explicitly and tells the AI to
turn the scene back into the dark rather than refuse coldly.

Three fields are OMITTED ENTIRELY to enforce this at the data layer
rather than by instruction alone. All three are optional and degrade
gracefully — verified against Code/Bottom Panel.txt:
- clothingStates — the prompt's CLOTHING STATES block is built only
  when the story declares it (the block is empty otherwise), tags are
  dropped by extractClothingTags(), and rollRandomClothingState()
  returns undefined. The AI is never told undress states exist.
- wetLocations / poolUniforms — the wet-outfit swap is guarded on both
  being present (line 4576), so no swap ever happens and every
  portrait comes from the four ordinary uniform categories.
- attractedTo — the "Attracted to: ..." clause is appended per NPC only
  when the field exists. No NPC here has one, so the AI is never nudged
  toward romance. likes/dislikes still drive affinity.

The Bathroom is deliberately NOT a wet location despite the manuscript
opening with a bath. The scene stays; the character is never rendered
undressed. If wet locations are ever added: Beach only, modest
swimwear written explicitly into every poolUniforms entry, Bathroom
excluded.

The standard minor-safety layer is unchanged and sits underneath all
of this — see Safety Layer.txt and Goals.txt SAFETY. Every character
is unambiguously adult, ages are stated (Jonah 19, player 21).

NPCs (12)
------------
Same authoring conventions as the other three: "identity" is the
Stage A baseline appearance line (clothing-free — clothing comes from
the Category), "bio" is who a character is, "personality" is how they
talk and carries the Hush pair.

Flat "npcs" array — no npcSets/openingScenes. The player is always the
Blythe daughter, so no gender-mirrored roster is needed and the
Meridian key-convention trap does not apply. Bios and personalities
say "sister" and "daughter" outright.

Gender split: 6 female, 6 male with a female player; 7 female, 5 male
with a male player, since the sibling mirrors.

THE HOUSE (3)
- The younger sibling, 19 — JONAH (brother) when the player is female,
  MARA (sister) when the player is male. Believes their brother or
  sister long before they believe in the thing, which is the
  distinction that matters. Each sleeps under a sound that carries
  through the wall — Jonah snores, Mara leaves a podcast running — so
  the sound stopping is how you know it has started on them. Mara's
  podcast is an accidental ward she does not know she has, the same
  mechanism as Ivy Ogle's radio. Category: family-male or
  family-female accordingly.
- Evelyn Blythe, 54 — Mam. Was Evelyn Fell, fifteen in 1989. Knows
  exactly what the Hush is and has never said a word, because she
  believes saying it out loud is how it finds you. She is wrong, and
  her silence is why it is still here. Category: family-female.
- Peter Blythe, 57 — Dad. Kind, practical, and already half taken.
  Everything he says is exactly what he would say, which is what makes
  him the most dangerous NPC in the house. Category: family-male.

THE CUL-DE-SAC (3)
- Ivy Ogle, 81 — number three. Knows every rule and gives them up one
  at a time in exchange for someone sitting down and listening
  properly. Not coy and not testing anyone; eighty-one and lonely.
  Category: village-female.
- Nate Ogle, 22 — her grandson, delighted rather than frightened.
  Believes the player instantly and for the wrong reasons, and belief
  that shallow does not hold. Category: village-male.
- Barry Kelso, 46 — the neighbour from the car argument, written to be
  the obvious sacrifice and written sympathetically enough that taking
  the offer should cost something. Category: village-male.

THE VILLAGE (4)
- Sheila Rudd, 63 — landlady of the Half Moon. Keeps her father's
  ledger of the village's dead, believes none of it, and has never in
  twenty-six years let a lone drinker walk home at closing. Category:
  village-female.
- Denny Aitchison, 78 — ex-pit deputy, has the plans, was on the 1968
  capping team, signed the sheet, has been waiting fifty-eight years
  for somebody to ask. Category: village-male.
- Kirsty Vane, 21 — best mate, Co-op, does not believe a word and
  turns up anyway. The only relationship with nothing supernatural in
  it, and the story is worse if she is converted into an investigator.
  Her presence is no protection and only the player knows that.
  Category: village-female.
- Yasmin Choudhury, 38 — GP. The rational pressure. Not a villain and
  right about almost everything. Telling her costs something real: it
  goes on a record and she telephones Evelyn. Category: village-female.

THE CHURCH (1)
- Nell Moore, 33 — priest at St Aidan's. Will not perform certainty
  she does not have, and will sit up all night with somebody, which
  turns out to be the actually useful sacrament. Category:
  village-female.

THE THING (1)
- The Marra — an NPC record so it has a personality field and can be
  placed in a scene, the way Solmere carries its shadowy faction. Its
  own uniform category, "entity", is a silhouette rather than clothes.
  promptRules and its bio both say USE IT SPARINGLY.

CAST WEIGHTING (stated in promptRules.setting — the engine has no
per-NPC tier field)
- Main: Jonah, Evelyn, Peter, Ivy, the Marra
- Strong supporting: Denny, Sheila, Kirsty, Nate
- Background: Barry, Yasmin, Nell

TOKENS
---------
The engine matches NPCs with a plain case-sensitive substring scan, no
word boundaries. All twelve tokens were checked programmatically at
build time against each other, against the thirteen location names, and
against a list of common capitalised words.

Four names were changed during authoring for collisions:
- Iris -> Ivy      (Iris fires inside "Irish")
- Frank -> Sheila  (Frank fires inside "Frankly")
- Ray -> Barry     (Ray fires inside "Rays")
- Cathal -> Nell   (changed for the gender balance, not a collision)

Jonah's room is called THE BOX ROOM rather than "Jonah's Room"
specifically so the location name does not carry his token and pull him
into every scene set there.

One residual: "Peter" fires inside "Peterlee" and "Peterhead", real
towns on this coast. Mitigated in fiction — promptRules.setting states
that no neighbouring town, city or county is ever named. If that ever
proves fragile, Dad is the easiest character in the cast to rename.

"Mara" and "Marra" do not contain one another (Marra's four-letter
substrings are "Marr" and "arra"), so the placeholder character name
cannot collide with the entity's token.

STANDARD UNIFORMS (Stage A baseline portraits)
---------------------------------------------------
Five categories, each combined with an NPC's identity line and a
generic neutral background at generation time — not written per-NPC,
so changing one line updates every NPC sharing that Category.
- family-male / family-female: home clothes in a cold house. Worn
  hoodies, old jeans or leggings, thick socks, nothing bought this
  decade.
- village-male / village-female: dressed for a wet north-east coast.
  Waterproofs and wax jackets over jumpers, sturdy boots, collar up.
- entity: no clothing and no surface. A flat black silhouette, long
  fingers, two red points where the eyes would be.
Every category line ends "fully clothed and modestly dressed at all
times, clearly an adult" — the equivalent slot where Solmere and
Halcyon put their sex-appeal phrasing.

playerCategoryTemplate: family-{playerGender}, which always resolves to
family-female (see THE PLAYER below).

THE PLAYER
-------------
The player is the elder Blythe child, 21, four months without proper
sleep. As of 24/08/2026 they choose which one:

  Female — the player is MARA, 21. Her brother JONAH is 19.
  Male   — the player is JONAH, 21. His sister MARA is 19.

The haunted role never moves. Only the name and sex of the person
holding it, and the sibling standing next to them, mirror. This was
weighed against staying female-only, which is what the manuscript does,
and the deciding argument was Evelyn: she was fifteen in 1989 when her
brother Duncan stopped sleeping, and the honest answer is she gave him
to it. Playing Mara makes the player Evelyn's echo — the sister who may
end up doing the same thing. Playing Jonah makes them DUNCAN's echo,
the one it came for, with a sister where Evelyn stood and Evelyn
herself watching a boy stop sleeping in her house for the second time
in her life. The rhyme does not break; it rotates, and the male reading
is arguably the crueller one. Nothing in the mechanic changes either
way, because being-believed already reverses the roles mid-story.

MECHANISM, all data, no engine work. npcSets and openingScenes are
existing gender-keyed fields (Meridian and Vermillion already use
both), resolved by resolveNpcSetsAndOpeningScene() at Begin. The two
casts are identical in eleven of twelve entries; only the sibling
differs. nameSets carries exactly one name per gender, so the Character
name field fills itself correctly and the player never types anything.

Hollowburn is the first Adventure to change WHO is in the cast rather
than only how they regard the player — Meridian and Vermillion keep the
same roster across both sets. Supported, but new, and worth knowing if
a cast bug ever shows up here first.

The playerGender setup field was already present and load-bearing
before this change, and still is: playerState.gender is read straight from
rawSetup.playerGender, and both playerIdentityWithAge() and
playerGenderPhrase() return an empty string when it is absent. Those
two functions are the fix for the wrong-sex baseline portrait — the
player's identity text is assembled from the Appearance dropdowns,
which describe hair, build and skin and never mention sex at all, so
without playerGenderPhrase() prepending "a woman" or "a man" the image
model has only indirect cues and guesses wrong often enough to matter.

Keeping the field also lets playerCategoryTemplate keep the standard
{playerGender} form rather than hardcoding the category; both
family-male and family-female uniforms already exist.

MAP LOCATIONS (13)
---------------------
NOT hub-and-spoke. Two hubs, because the story is about a house and
the village that put it there. The front door is drawn as the single
solid line joining them; everything else is dashed.

House lobe, hub = THE LANDING (the crossroads of the house, and where
it waits):
- Your Bedroom (starting location)
- The Landing (hub)
- The Bathroom
- The Box Room (Jonah's)
- Mam and Dad's Room
- The Kitchen
- The Sitting Room
- The Back Garden

Village lobe, hub = THE CUL-DE-SAC:
- The Cul-de-sac (hub)
- The Half Moon Inn
- St Aidan's
- The Beach

Danger site (red, dashed, path drawn severed — same treatment as
Halcyon's Sunken Villa):
- The Old Pit Head

The SVG was generated programmatically. All thirteen rect ids were
verified against locationSlug() output, and checked for overlapping
boxes and out-of-viewBox placement, before delivery. It was also
rendered in a browser and inspected — which caught a real defect: the
raw ampersand in the "Mam & Dad's" label is invalid XML and silently
killed the whole render until it was escaped. Any future map with an
ampersand in a label needs the same escaping.

No wetLocations.

ADVENTURE SETUP FIELDS (Hollowburn-specific)
-----------------------------------------------
The setup screen is shown again as of 24/08/2026, because the
Male/Female choice is a real question the Adventure has to ask. It was
briefly skipped entirely (23/08/2026, `skipSetup: true`), and that flag
is now off on BOTH sides — the hosted JSON and the ADVENTURES registry
entry — since either one alone still skips the screen. Every field
except Gender fills itself, so the screen is one click if the player
does not care, and one real decision if they do.

- How Long Since You Slept — A few weeks / Three months / Since the
  funeral / You can't remember. Sets starting fragility. "Since the
  funeral" is deliberately unattached to any named death so the
  narration AI can decide whose it was. Rolled at random per story.
- What You Believe — Sceptic / Sensitive / Devout / You already know
  it's real. Steers how quickly the player can accept what is
  happening and how the village reads them. Rolled at random per
  story.

Plus the universal fields. Gender is two tiles, Female and Male, with
no default — it is the one thing worth choosing (see THE PLAYER).
Character name is left blank and drawn from nameSets, which holds
exactly one name per gender, so it fills in as Mara or Jonah without
the player typing. Age is 21. The Appearance dropdowns default to
short straight, dark brown, average build, fair — changed 24/08/2026
from shoulder-length black, which read as female-specific once a male
player was possible; it matches Jonah's canon look and stays perfectly
plausible for Mara. No lore describes the player's appearance, so
nothing constrains this. No freeform custom fields.

Gender is not enforced. Begin stays enabled before a tile is picked, so
clicking straight through rolls one at random — the same "nothing needs
entering" convention every other Adventure follows. Making it the one
required field would need an engine change.

LORE FILES (19)
------------------
setting, locations, the-hush, the-marra, being-believed,
the-pit-and-the-shaft, the-blythe-house, and twelve npc-* files.
loreEntries in the JSON point at the GitHub raw paths for all nineteen.

npc-jonah-blythe.txt is labelled "The Blythe Children" and covers both
siblings as a pair, stating which one the player is under each gender.
loreEntries is a flat list with no gender keying — unlike npcSets and
openingScenes — so a per-gender sibling file is not possible, and one
file covering both is the fix. The FILENAME still says jonah, which is
now slightly wrong; it was kept deliberately so the change is a single
overwrite rather than a delete-and-re-add, which on GitHub's web
uploader leaves a window where the live app 404s.

OPEN / NOT YET DECIDED
---------------------------
- imageStyle is "painted illustration, muted desaturated palette,
  overcast north-east English light, front on shot" rather than the
  "painted anime style, front on shot" that Solmere, Meridian and
  Halcyon all share. This is the single most likely field to need
  reverting if the portraits come out wrong — it is the only place
  this Adventure departs from a setting proven against the live image
  pipeline.
- Whether Evelyn's price was her body (the arthritis) or her brother
  (Duncan, 1989). The lore currently implies the second and leaves the
  first available as a lie she tells herself. Duncan is stronger and
  gives the missing-photograph motif something to do; it also makes
  her much harder to forgive.
- Whether Duncan Fell should exist as a thirteenth NPC record who only
  appears late, worn like a coat. Rosters are static, so he would sit
  in the cast from turn one doing nothing — the argument against.
- A wanted fourteenth location, "the Long Landing" — the house's other
  version, appearing on the map only after the player has been in the
  Hush. Needs the hidden "Unknown" areas feature, which is
  brainstormed and not built. Omitted for now; it works as pure
  narration.
- No titleImageUrl or narrationBackdropUrl set (both optional).
