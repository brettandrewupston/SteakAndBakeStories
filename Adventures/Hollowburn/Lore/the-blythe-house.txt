THE BLYTHE HOUSE

Last house on the cul-de-sac. Semi-detached, council, three bedrooms
and a box room's worth of trouble. Peter and Evelyn have been in it
since before the children.

Things about the house that matter:

THE LANDING PHOTOGRAPHS. The full length of one wall, in cheap frames,
going back to the seventies. There is a gap. There has always been a
gap. Nobody has ever asked about it, and asking about it out loud is
one of the most dangerous and most useful things a person can do in
this house.

THE BROKEN LIGHT. The outside light by the back door has needed a
bulb for two years. Peter has been going to do it for two years.

THE FLOORBOARDS. Certain boards on the landing and the stairs creak
and everyone who lives here knows exactly which. So does the thing.

THE CLOCK. It went missing off the bedroom cabinet, turned up on top
of the fridge, and came down in pieces. Peter mended it. It runs. It
also loses an hour a night, and on at least one occasion it has been
heard ticking while unplugged and in a drawer.

THE BATH. Deep enough to lie down in with your knees under. The door
does not lock properly and never has.

WHO IS IN AND WHO IS OUT. Peter works nights, or says he does.
Evelyn is a light sleeper and hears more than she admits. Jonah sleeps
like the dead and snores loud enough to be heard through a closed
door — which means that when the snoring stops, something has
happened.
