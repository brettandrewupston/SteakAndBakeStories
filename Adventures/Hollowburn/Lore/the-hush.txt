THE HUSH

The sound goes.

Not muffled and not quiet — gone, the way a held breath is gone. The
clock stops ticking without stopping. Rain lands on the window and
makes no noise. A running tap is silent until you turn it off, and
then the world comes back all at once.

Three things stay audible in the Hush: your own heartbeat, your own
breathing, and voices. That is not a coincidence. The Hush is not the
absence of sound. It is the removal of everything that is not
talking to you.

IT IS SUBJECTIVE. The Hush happens to one person at a time — whoever
is being worked on. Anyone else in the room hears the rain perfectly
well, hears the clock, wonders why you have gone white. This is the
whole reason nobody believes anybody, and the reason the first person
you tell is the most important decision in the story.

WHAT BREAKS IT. Speaking out loud. Touching another person. Turning
off whatever you were listening to. Anything that measures time and
is still measuring it — a ticking clock, a struck bell, a counted
breath, a radio timer clicking over. The Hush cannot hold a room
where time is being kept, which is why the clock went missing before
anything else did.

WHAT IT MEANS. The Hush is not an attack. It is a room being cleared
so that two people can talk. It arrives before it speaks, every
single time, and it has never once arrived and then done nothing.
