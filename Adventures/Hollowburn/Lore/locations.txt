LOCATIONS

Two halves. Upstairs and downstairs are one world, reached through
THE LANDING. The village is the other, reached through THE CUL-DE-SAC.
The front door is the only join, and the player has to cross it on
foot, at whatever hour it happens to be.

THE HOUSE

YOUR BEDROOM — At the end of the landing. A bed, a cabinet where a
clock used to sit, a bookcase, a window that looks over the cul-de-sac
and a collared dove nesting in the eaves outside it. The door is left
open a crack out of habit. The starting location.

THE LANDING — The top of the stairs and the crossroads of the house.
Four doors and a window that lets the moon in. The whole length of one
wall is family photographs. This is where the house is coldest and
where the thing waits.

THE BATHROOM — Old, small, a bath deep enough to lie down in. Steam
that clouds the mirror. The door does not lock properly.

THE BOX ROOM — Jonah's room, directly at the top of the stairs. A
television, a games console, a permanently unmade bed, a drying rack
of laundry nobody has ever put away.

MAM AND DAD'S ROOM — Their door squeaks. Evelyn's perfume, the good
one, only comes out when Peter is taking her somewhere.

THE KITCHEN — Under-cupboard lights that barely reach the corners.
The fridge that the clock was found on top of. The back door, and the
outside light beside it that has needed a bulb for two years.

THE SITTING ROOM — Where the family watch films together with the
popcorn out. Peter's chair. A doorframe that somebody looked around,
once.

THE BACK GARDEN — Overgrown, a rotary line, a fence panel down. Cold
even in August. You can hear the sea from here if the road is quiet.

THE VILLAGE

THE CUL-DE-SAC — Eight houses and a turning circle. Barry Kelso's
drive, Ivy Ogle's at number three with the light always on, and a dog
somewhere that howls most nights and nobody will admit to owning.

THE HALF MOON INN — The last pub. Pool table, carpet, a fire lit even
in summer. Sheila Rudd behind the bar and a ledger under it.

ST AIDAN'S — The village church. Cold, mostly empty, unlocked at
hours it should not be, because Nell Moore keeps forgetting.

THE BEACH — Ten minutes down the bank. Grey sand, wind, the pit's old
workings running out for miles underneath the water.

THE OLD PIT HEAD — Fenced, signposted, and gone through by everyone
in the village at some point in their teens. The winding gear is
scrap. The shaft is capped. What is under the cul-de-sac is not the
shaft.
