THE ARTICLES (PERPETUA) — ADVENTURE CONTENT
===========================================================
Last updated: 23/08/2026
Seventh Adventure for the Story Generator. Architecture/decisions live in
the parent folder's Goals.txt and Features.txt — this file is just this
Adventure's canon (premise, rules, NPCs, map, setup fields).

Adventure id: perpetua. Display title: "The Articles". Files are named
Perpetua.* to match the id, consistent with Solmere / Meridian / Halcyon
/ Hollowburn / Pemberton / Gallant.

CANON ONLY as of 23/08/2026. No Story Definition JSON, no lore files, no
map SVG, no UI skin, nothing uploaded to R2, and NOT REGISTERED in the
ADVENTURES array in Code/Bottom Panel.txt. This file is the brief the
data will be built from. See OUTSTANDING at the end.

PREMISE
----------
The Perpetua is a two-hundred-ton pirate sloop working the Tallows, a
chain of low green islands nobody's king can hold and everybody's king
claims.

She is not commanded. She is constituted. The Articles are nailed to the
mast and signed by every hand aboard, and they say what everything is
worth: a share of the take, a hundred pieces for a lost hand, one vote
each. The Captain commands in chase and in battle and at no other time.
The Quartermaster divides the plunder, hears every dispute and carries
out every punishment, and can overrule the Captain anywhere except a
chase. Everything else — the course, the prize, the punishment, and who
is Captain by the end of the month — is put to the company and counted.

The player is a new hand who has just signed. One vote, same as
everybody. No rank to inherit and none to be given.

No real nation, sea or year is ever named. The King is only ever the
King, and the Tallows are the only place on the map with a name.

WHAT EVERYBODY KNOWS
-----------------------
There is no secret aboard the Perpetua and no plot to uncover.

Every crew in the Tallows knows the Perpetua is one bad season from
voting Thomasin Creed out, because everybody says so, at the capstan, to
her face, and she says it back. A mutiny here is not a betrayal. It is
the constitution working as designed, out loud, in front of the person
it is about.

This is the Adventure's central rule and it is load-bearing: nothing
here is done behind anyone's back, because the machinery for doing it in
front of them already exists and is faster. Every scene that tries to
become a conspiracy resolves in one line — somebody calls a vote and it
is counted. The story is not an intrigue and must never become one. It
is an argument about how to run a ship, held by people who cannot get
off it.

The consequence for play is that the pressure is always present and
always speakable. There is always a vote pending or one just resolved,
which gives the narration AI a concrete thing every scene can bend
toward instead of a secret it has to withhold.

TONE
-------
Loud, filthy, funny, and physically dangerous. Rope burn, salt sores,
bad water, and a fight roughly once a month that some of these people
will not walk away from. Warm about the company and unsentimental about
what it is doing for a living.

Romance runs the length of it and it is romance in a place with no
privacy at all: eighty people, one deck to sleep on, and everybody
already knows.

THE VOTE (signature mechanic)
--------------------------------
Nothing aboard is decided by rank. Every question of consequence is put
to the company, which means the player can never order anyone to do
anything and can only ever get them on side, and it means every scene is
worth something later. A favour done on the gun deck in Act I is a hand
in the air in Act III.

Implemented with no engine changes: the relationship system IS the vote.
The relationship ladder is authored as the Articles' own language (see
RELATIONSHIP LABELS), so the number the engine already tracks and
already displays is a count of who would back the player. A vote
resolves by reading the room the engine is already modelling — no new
field, no new persisted state, no tally to maintain.

promptRules.setting carries the rule that any hand may call a vote at
the capstan, that a vote is counted rather than argued to a conclusion,
that the Captain's authority ends when the chase does, and that the
narrator counts the standing relationship tiers when a vote is called
rather than inventing a result.

CONTENT
----------
Halcyon level in the prose. Deliberately below Gallant in the portraits:
the register here is bare, salt-stained and worked-in rather than
engineered, and it is diegetic — this is the tropics, there is no fresh
water to spare for washing, clothing comes off dead men and is cut down
to fit, and nobody aboard has been alone since they signed.

Flirtation, jealousy, and decisions made in a hammock four feet from
someone else's are ordinary texture rather than exceptions, and are
written freely and explicitly when a scene goes there; escalation within
a scene still follows the player's lead. All characters are written
unambiguously adult.

Three complications the story treats as real rather than ignoring:

- Adaeze Nwoye administers punishment. If the player is ever punished
  under the Articles, she is the one who does it, and the story does not
  arrange for her to be elsewhere that day.
- Rasmus Kyd signed on the same day as the player and is competing for
  the same votes. The story does not resolve this by making him a
  villain.
- Isaac Pell deserted the King's service. Anyone aboard could sell him
  for a pardon at Kingsreach, and he has been aboard four years without
  it happening, which is not the same as being safe.

Perrine Vaudray and Obadiah Finch sit outside the flirtation frame
entirely; their attractedTo fields are omitted, which suppresses the
per-NPC "Attracted to: ..." clause for those two only. Every other NPC
carries one.

The standard minor-safety layer is unchanged and sits underneath all of
this — see Safety Layer.txt and Goals.txt SAFETY.

NPCs (15)
------------
Same authoring conventions as the other six: "identity" is the Stage A
baseline appearance line (clothing-free — clothing comes from the
Category), "bio" is who a character is, "personality" is how they talk.
Every character is a clearly adult person.

Flat "npcs" array — no npcSets/openingScenes. The player's gender does
not change the roster, so the gender-mirrored mechanism Meridian needs
is not applicable and the npcSets key-convention trap does not apply.

Gender split: 8 female, 7 male.

ELECTED (4)
- Thomasin Creed — Captain. Elected two years ago on the back of one
  enormous prize and has taken nothing worth having since. Commands in
  chase and battle and is a passenger with a better cabin the rest of
  the time. Theatrical, brave, generous, genuinely brilliant for the
  ninety minutes a month it matters, and running out of arithmetic.
  Category: captain-female.
- Adaeze Nwoye — Quartermaster. Divides every share, hears every
  dispute, carries out every punishment, and can overrule the Captain
  anywhere but a chase. Exactly fair, which the crew have learned is not
  the same thing as kind. The slow burn. Category: officer-female.
- Ilse Halvorsen — Boatswain. Runs the deck, the work and the rota, and
  gives the player every foul job aboard for the first month on
  principle rather than malice. Loud, enormous fun, obscene, and the
  first person to defend the player to anyone who is not present.
  Category: officer-female.
- Yusuf Baraka — Sailing Master. The only person aboard who can
  navigate, which makes him the one man no vote can touch and which
  frightens him more than it comforts him. Precise, teetotal, keeps a
  private journal. Category: officer-male.

BEFORE THE MAST (6)
- Mina Roux — master gunner. Half-deaf from her own guns, obsessive
  about them, the best shot in the Tallows, and the reason the Perpetua
  wins fights she should lose. Wants the Captaincy and says so at the
  capstan, because saying so at the capstan is how it is done here.
  Category: crew-female.
- Fenwick Cray — carpenter. Keeps a rotten hull afloat with no materials
  and no complaint. Vast, gentle, slow to anger and then not, and
  catastrophically in love with someone he will not name. Category:
  crew-male.
- Ambrose Lambe — surgeon. A barber with ambitions, one anatomy book and
  a genuinely good hand. Cheerful about amputation in a way that is
  either steadying or appalling depending on the day. Category:
  crew-male.
- Isaac Pell — topman, deserted from the King's service four years ago.
  The only person aboard who has been on the other side of this, which
  the company has never entirely finished deciding about. Quiet,
  capable, watchful, and the best judge of a fight on the ship.
  Category: crew-male.
- Odalys Ferrer — cook, and by long custom the ship's memory. Four
  captains sailed with, three buried. Feeds everybody and reads
  everybody, and is the person to ask what the company actually thinks
  before spending a vote finding out. Category: crew-female.
- Rasmus Kyd — signed the Articles the same hour as the player. Charming,
  quick, cheerfully self-interested, and the direct rival: every vote he
  wins is one the player did not. Not a villain and never written as
  one — just faster, and often right. Category: crew-male.

THE TALLOWS (5)
- Perrine Vaudray — runs the market at Cutwater Bay. Buys anything, sets
  the price, and is therefore the person who decides what a prize was
  actually worth. Not a pirate, and richer than every pirate in the
  chain. Category: shore-female.
- Hetty Bramble — keeps the Ropewalk, where crews are made and unmade
  and where half the Perpetua's company was recruited drunk. Knows every
  hand in the Tallows and whose Articles they are on. Category:
  shore-female.
- Obadiah Finch — the Tallows' only lawyer. Writes Articles for a fee,
  and will read the player's own back to them word for word on the day
  they break them. Dry, delighted by all of it, entirely uninterested in
  whether anybody hangs. Category: shore-male.
- Loveday Ashe — Captain of the Kestrel, and the Adventure's standing
  argument against its own premise. Runs her ship as an autocracy,
  appoints instead of electing, and takes more, faster, with fewer
  people killed. Charming, direct, and openly recruiting. Category:
  captain-female.
- Aurelian Stack — Commodore, the King's man in these waters. Patient,
  competent, personally decent, and will hang every named person in this
  file and be sorry about it afterwards. Not a monster and must never be
  written as one. Category: king-male.

CAST WEIGHTING (stated in promptRules.setting — the engine has no
per-NPC tier field)
- Main: Adaeze, Thomasin, Rasmus, Mina, Odalys, Loveday
- Strong supporting: Ilse, Yusuf, Isaac, Fenwick, Perrine, Aurelian
- Background: Ambrose, Hetty, Obadiah

TOKENS
---------
The engine matches NPCs with a plain case-sensitive substring scan, no
word boundaries. All fifteen tokens are to be checked programmatically
at build time against each other, against the thirteen location names,
and against a list of common capitalised words, as with Pemberton and
Gallant.

This Adventure carries a hazard the others do not: ordinary shipboard
vocabulary is capitalised at the start of a sentence constantly — Watch,
Deck, Mast, Sail, Port, Bell, Shot, Hold — so no token may be a working
noun, and the location names have to be swept against narration as well
as against the cast.

One name is already changed at brief stage for a collision:
- Will -> Fenwick. "Will" fires inside "William" and, fatally, opens a
  sentence every few paragraphs ("Will you...", "Will he..."). His token
  is Fenwick; the bio may still call him Will in dialogue, because
  dialogue is not scanned for tokens, only npc.token is matched.

Residuals to confirm at build, all judged low risk: "Mina" inside
"Wilhelmina" (the full name is not used anywhere), "Isaac" (no common
host word found), "Stack" is not a token — Aurelian Stack's token is
"Aurelian" precisely because "Stack" opens sentences about cargo.

Ship names are not tokens — the engine matches npc.token only — so
"Perpetua", "Kestrel" and "Absalom" carry no matching risk and can be
used freely in narration.

STANDARD UNIFORMS (Stage A baseline portraits)
---------------------------------------------------
Eight categories, each combined with an NPC's identity line and a
generic neutral background at generation time — not written per-NPC, so
changing one line updates every NPC sharing that Category.

The register is heat, salt and hard physical work rather than
engineered display: sleeves gone, shirts open or off, everything cut
down from clothes taken off somebody else, sun on skin, sweat, bare
feet on tar. Both genders are written with the same emphasis — the male
categories are as exposed and as worked as the female ones, not a
modest counterpart to them. It sits below Gallant's costume set
deliberately; the fan service here comes from the climate and the total
absence of privacy, not from a costume designed to be photographed.

Identity lines are written to match. Since clothing comes from the
Category, each NPC's identity line carries an explicit adult figure
description — build, bust, waist, hips, legs — plus the marks of the
work: rope scars, powder burns, sun lines, old breaks badly set.

Every category carries "fully clothed, clearly an adult", and
clothingStates handles everything past baseline. That pairing is the
entire guardrail and neither half is optional at any heat level. The
minor-safety layer underneath is untouched by any of it.

- crew-male / crew-female: prize clothes cut down to fit. A linen shirt
  with the sleeves hacked off, open to the waist or knotted under the
  chest, bare midriff and bare arms, sailcloth slops cut short and
  belted low on the hips with a length of rope, bare legs, bare feet,
  a knife on a lanyard, sun-bleached and salt-stiff and worn to
  transparency in places, everything fitted close because it has been
  re-sewn by hand to fit a body it was not made for.
- officer-male / officer-female: the same clothes with one good coat
  over them, taken off a prize and kept for the look of the thing —
  worn open over bare skin, sleeves turned back, nothing underneath
  buttoned, a sash at the waist pulled tight, boots instead of bare
  feet. Better than everybody else's by exactly one garment, which is
  the entire point of an elected office aboard this ship.
- captain-female: a genuinely fine coat, tailored, and nothing under it
  that a tailor would recognise — shirt open to the sash, waist drawn
  in, cut high on the thigh over long boots, a hat that cost more than
  the coat. Theatre, deliberately, because a captain in the Tallows is
  a performance of confidence and both women in this category know it.
- shore-male / shore-female: Tallows town clothes, thin and light for
  the heat — a shift or shirt worn off the shoulder, laced loose,
  skirts kirtled up out of the mud or breeches rolled to the knee, bare
  arms, bare feet, a great deal of gold worn where it can be seen.
- king-male: the King's uniform, immaculate and entirely wrong for this
  climate — full coat, full facings, gorget, everything buttoned,
  sweating through it and refusing to acknowledge it. Aurelian only,
  following Pemberton's maintenance-male and Gallant's exec-female
  precedent for a single-member category.

wetLocations and poolUniforms are BOTH present — the first use outside
Halcyon. Careening Beach and The Bone Shoals are the wet locations, and
the swap is the working state: stripped to a shift or breeches, soaked,
hove down in the shallows with the ship on her side. The swap is
guarded on both fields being present, so this is a deliberate opt-in
rather than the inert omission Gallant chose.

clothingStates is retained at the Solmere/Meridian level.

playerCategoryTemplate: crew-{playerGender}. The player is always a hand
before the mast, so like Halcyon, Pemberton and Gallant the template has
a single variable — and it stays crew-* in the Elected ending, because
the Articles do not issue the Captain a different shirt.

MAP LOCATIONS (13)
---------------------
Hub-and-spoke from The Waist, with the Ropewalk as the strong second hub
ashore — the same shape as Halcyon's Pool Deck, Pemberton's Kitchen and
Gallant's Roof.

- The Capstan (starting location — where the Articles are signed and
  every vote is counted)
- The Waist (hub — the open main deck, where the whole company ends up)
- The Berth Deck (hammocks touching, no privacy, everybody hears
  everything and mentions it in the morning)
- The Great Cabin (the Captain's, and by the Articles not hers alone —
  any hand may enter it, which is the single line of the Articles that
  most annoys every captain who has ever held it)
- The Gun Deck (Mina's, warm, and the only part of the ship anybody
  maintains properly)
- The Hold (the shares, the water, the rats, the dark)
- The Tops (aloft — the only place on the ship where two people can be
  alone, which everybody below understands perfectly)
- The Galley (Odalys's, and the ship's real parliament — every vote is
  decided here and merely counted at the capstan)
- Cutwater Bay (the market — Perrine's, and where a prize becomes money)
- The Ropewalk (second hub — Hetty's, where crews are made and unmade)
- Careening Beach (where the ship is hove down on her side for a week at
  a time: no guns, no escape, no prizes, and nothing to do but argue)
- Kingsreach (the King's port — the assizes, the gallows, and the only
  place in the chain to buy powder legally)
- The Bone Shoals (red, dashed, path drawn severed — same treatment as
  Halcyon's Sunken Villa, Pemberton's Overflow and Gallant's Blackout
  Block)

THE BONE SHOALS
------------------
A shoal that eats ships. It is charted, it is marked, and every hand in
the Tallows knows exactly what is there, which is: the Absalom, and
forty-one people, and a prize on the far side that is still there
because the far side is what nobody reaches.

It holds no secret and the lore states plainly what happened. The
Absalom's company voted to try it. The vote was properly called and
properly counted and the majority were wrong, and there is no clause in
any set of Articles anywhere against being wrong.

It fills the same structural slot as the other Adventures' restricted
site without reintroducing the investigation plot this Adventure
deliberately does not have. What is at the Bone Shoals is not a mystery.
It is the price of the premise, sitting on the map, in red.

THE CAREEN
-------------
The clock. A hull in these waters fouls in about ten weeks, and a fouled
hull cannot catch anything, which means the Perpetua must be hove down
and scraped or she stops being a pirate ship and becomes eighty people
on a slow boat. Careening costs a week beached and defenceless, no
prizes, no shares, and a company with nothing to do but count what they
have not earned.

It is not a turn count and never resolves on one. It resolves on
milestones, and its function is pressure: every argument aboard gets
worse as the hull gets worse, and everybody can see the weed on the
waterline.

THE ARC
----------
Three acts, ten milestones, six endings. The acts turn on milestones,
never on turn count.

ACT I — NEW HAND, until first_prize. The player has signed but has not
yet stood in a fight, and nobody in the Tallows spends a vote on someone
who has not.

ACT II — FULL SHARE, until the_question. The player has a share and a
voice, and both are now worth courting — by Rasmus, by Mina, and
eventually by people with worse ideas.

ACT III — THE QUESTION. A vote has been called that nobody aboard can
abstain from, and every scene bends toward the count.

MILESTONES
- first_prize — the player is aboard for a prize taken and divided.
  Fires on the division, not the fight, because the division is where
  this Adventure's fights actually happen. Closes Act I.
- spoke_first (needs first_prize) — the player speaks at the capstan and
  is heard out rather than shouted down. The moment they become a vote
  somebody has to win rather than a hand somebody counts.
- the_punishment — a member of the company is punished under the
  Articles by vote, and the player voted. The first time the
  constitution costs somebody something in front of them, carried out
  by Adaeze, in daylight, in front of everyone.
- the_share — the player learns what a share is actually worth after
  Perrine's price, the surgeon's fund and the compensation clauses come
  out of it. The Adventure's money moment and the reason every later
  decision is about a number.
- the_kestrel — the Perpetua meets Loveday Ashe. An autocracy
  outperforms the vote in front of the whole company and there is no
  good answer to it, and the story does not supply one.
- the_chart (needs the_share) — the player learns to navigate, from
  Yusuf or from his charts without asking. It is the only thing aboard
  that makes a person unvotable, everybody notices them doing it, and
  what it costs is Yusuf.
- careened — the ship is hove down. A week ashore with no guns, no
  income and every unresolved argument in the same three hundred yards
  of sand.
- called (needs spoke_first) — the player calls a vote themselves. Not
  necessarily wins it; calling one and losing it is a distinct and
  survivable outcome that the story treats as ordinary.
- the_question — the vote to put Thomasin out is called, and the player's
  name is one of the alternatives. Closes Act II. No structural
  prerequisite, deliberately, for the same reason Pemberton's review_set
  and Gallant's the_offer have none: an ending a passive player can be
  locked out of is an Adventure that cannot end.
- the_count (needs the_question) — the vote is counted at the capstan.

ENDINGS
- Elected (the_count:player) — the player is Captain. Command in chase
  and battle and nothing else, the person who held it last week is still
  aboard and still has a vote, and everyone remembers exactly whose hand
  went up. Written as a job, not a triumph.
- Confirmed (the_count:thomasin) — the vote fails and Thomasin keeps it.
  Nobody is exiled, nothing is forgiven quickly, and the player goes
  back to a berth deck where half the hammocks voted the other way.
- The Kestrel — the player leaves for Loveday's ship. Better food,
  better prizes, more money, no vote. Written as a genuine improvement,
  which is the entire reason it hurts.
- Marooned — the Articles' own punishment, reachable at any point, on a
  real breach properly voted by the company. A key, a pistol, one shot,
  and Adaeze rowing back.
- Kingsreach — taken and tried. The King's pardon is real, it is
  available, and it costs exactly one list of names.
- The Shoals — the company votes to try the Bone Shoals. The player
  votes with them, or votes against and goes anyway, because that is
  what signing meant. A real death ending, and reachable by a majority
  the player argued for.

No ending abolishes the Articles and none is written as one. Whoever is
Captain at the end, the paper on the mast outlasts them.

TIME PHASES
--------------
A watch, not a calendar day, following Halcyon's shooting-day,
Pemberton's office-day and Gallant's shift precedent: Morning Watch /
Forenoon / Dog Watch / First Watch / Middle Watch.

The Dog Watch is the ship's social hour by long custom — short, shared,
everyone on deck — and is where most of the Adventure's group scenes
belong.

startingPhase: Forenoon — the player signs at the capstan with the
company watching, and is at work ten minutes later.

RELATIONSHIP LABELS
----------------------
Seven tiers on the engine's min-threshold form, written as the vote, so
that the number the engine tracks and the number the fiction turns on
are the same number. Bottom is not Nemesis and top is not Love, the same
move Hollowburn, Pemberton and Gallant make.

0 Would See You Marooned / 15 Votes Against You / 35 Signed The Same
Paper / 50 Hears You Out / 65 Votes With You / 80 Speaks For You /
95 Would Hang Beside You.

Aurelian Stack uses the same scale and it means something entirely
different coming from him: a Commodore who would speak for the player is
a Commodore who will say something kind at the trial.

ADVENTURE SETUP FIELDS (Perpetua-specific)
----------------------------------------------
- What You Were Before — canon for that playthrough, and the thing the
  company will bring up whenever it is inconvenient:
  - The King's service, and you left it without permission.
  - A merchant's clerk. You can read, write and add, which is rarer
    aboard than courage.
  - A fisherman. You are the only person here who understands the sea
    as weather rather than as a road.
  - Taken off a prize six months ago and given the choice. You took it.
  - A plantation, and you are not going back.
  - Born to it. Your mother signed Articles and so did hers.
- Your Trade — what the company voted you aboard for: Gun crew /
  Topman / Carpenter's mate / Surgeon's mate / Powder and stores /
  Nothing in particular, they were short of hands.
- What You Signed For — The share / A pardon you intend to earn /
  Somebody already aboard / You had nowhere else to be. Odalys Ferrer
  will have worked out which within a fortnight and will not say so
  until it matters.

Plus the required universal fields: Character name, Gender (both
options), the four Appearance dropdowns, and Age. No freeform custom
fields — curated fields only, per Adventure. Gallant's open question
about free-text naming does not arise here: nothing on this setup screen
puts a player-authored string into narration.

IMAGE SETTINGS
-----------------
imageStyle: "painted anime style, front on shot" — the setting proven
against the live image pipeline by Solmere, Meridian, Halcyon and
Pemberton, and kept by Gallant. Hollowburn's departure from it is
flagged in its own outline as the field most likely to need reverting,
so this Adventure does not repeat the experiment.

baselineBackground: the standard plain neutral studio line.

poseVocabulary is shipboard and written to the same register as the
uniforms: hauling on a line with the whole body, straddling a gun,
sitting in the rigging with legs hanging, leaning back against the mast
with arms up, kneeling on wet planking scrubbing, drinking straight from
the jug with the head back, stripped to the waist working in the sun,
hove down in the shallows soaked through, standing at the capstan
addressing the company, holding a hand up for a vote.

expressionVocabulary gains "salt-burned", "wary", "delighted",
"seasick", "flushed", "counting the room".

OPEN / NOT YET DECIDED
---------------------------
- Whether the vote tally should appear in the story export. The mechanic
  is entirely narrator-side, which means a tester who thinks a vote
  resolved wrongly has nothing to look at and nothing to send back.
  Putting the count and the per-NPC tiers the narrator used into the
  export at the moment a vote resolves is the cheapest way to make this
  Adventure's signature mechanic falsifiable, and it is the one thing in
  this brief that may need engine support.
- Whether Loveday Ashe's autocracy should ever visibly fail. Current
  position: no. The moment the vote is proved right by outcome, the
  Adventure becomes propaganda for its own premise and stops being an
  argument. Same reasoning as Clarence in Pemberton and the A-list in
  Gallant, and the same counter-argument applies: a rival who is never
  humbled reads to some players as an unfinished thread.
- Whether the player can actually become Captain. Current position: yes,
  and Elected is written as a job rather than a reward, because the
  alternative — a player who can only ever be kingmaker — makes the
  central mechanic something that happens to other people.
- Whether Yusuf Baraka is genuinely irreplaceable. Current position: yes
  for the whole Adventure unless the player takes the_chart, and the
  file should not hint at a second navigator. The cost of the_chart is
  what it does to him, not a plot obstacle it removes.
- Whether the player's own Articles breach should ever be forgiven
  without a vote. Current position: no. Adaeze does not have that power
  and inventing it for a sympathetic moment would cost the Adventure the
  only rule it has.
- What is on the other side of the Bone Shoals. Unwritten on purpose.
  The Tallows chain is the whole map.
- No titleImageUrl, coverUrl or narrationBackdropUrl decided.
- No UI skin designed. The obvious direction is the Articles themselves
  — a nailed-up sheet, ruled columns, signatures and marks against
  numbered clauses, tarred rope, sailcloth and iron. It would be the
  seventh skin and the first one that is a document rather than a place
  or a piece of software.
