THE NIGHT — 31 DECEMBER 1949

This is the spine of the case. These times are established and true in every
version of the story. What is not established is who is lying about where
they were in them, and all nine of them are.

11:20  Delphine Rosetti's second set ends. The chorus line comes off. The
       floor is at capacity — eight hundred and eleven covers, Vivienne's
       count.
11:32  The Cardinal goes up to six alone. Two waiters see him at the lift.
       He is in a good mood and stops to tell one of them so.
11:35  A tray goes up on the service dumbwaiter: a bottle and TWO glasses.
       He asked for two. This is the single most important fact in the case
       and nobody downstairs thought anything of it at the time.
11:40  The band starts the countdown build. It is loud enough on six that
       you can feel it through the floor.
11:47  The shot. Nobody hears it. The medical examiner at Carbone and Sons
       will fix this time on the second day; before that it is Joey Marsh's
       estimate and she says so.
11:52  Midnight is called four minutes early by a bandleader who has been
       drinking. Vivienne notices the Cardinal is not on the mezzanine for
       the toast, which he has never once missed.
11:58  Josephine Marsh opens the office door with the master key. The brass
       bolt is thrown on the inside and the door opens anyway, which is the
       first thing the Sparrow should be told and the last thing anybody
       thinks to mention.
11:54  — see Josephine Marsh's file. There is a call logged from the office
       telephone at this time and it is four minutes before she says she got
       in.
00:04  The Sparrow arrives.

WHAT THE ROOM LOOKED LIKE
The Cardinal in his chair, one shot, close. The pistol from his own drawer
on the blotter, wiped. Two glasses: one poured and untouched, one clean. The
Modigliani swung out and the safe standing open and empty. A cigarette in
the ashtray, half smoked, with lipstick on it. A cufflink under the desk. A
single white ostrich feather on the landing carpet outside.

WHAT THE ROOM DOES NOT SHOW
No struggle. No forced entry. Nothing taken but the book. And a bolt thrown
from the inside of a room nobody was in.
