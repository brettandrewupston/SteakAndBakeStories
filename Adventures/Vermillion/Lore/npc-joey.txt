JOSEPHINE "JOEY" MARSH

Mistress of the casino floor, 35. Runs the pit, hires every dealer,
carries every key in the building. She found the body at 11:58 with her
master key and telephoned the Sparrow before she telephoned one single
person in the Family, which is either the smartest thing anybody did that
night or the last thing she will ever get away with. Her papers say
Josephine Marsh of Baltimore. Her papers are eleven years old and cost
sixty dollars.

THE LIE
She says she went up on a hunch at about ten to twelve. She went up at
11:43, and she did not go on a hunch — the Cardinal's bell rang the pit
and stopped in the middle of the ring, which the whole pit heard and which
she has told nobody.

THE GAP
11:43 to 11:58.

THE TRACE
The master key, obviously. And her prints on the dial of an open safe,
which she does not deny for a second, and a call logged from the office
telephone at 11:54 — four minutes before the time she says she got the
door open.

THE MOTIVE
There is a file on the Cardinal's shelf with her real name on the spine.
She has known it was there for eleven years and has been very good and
very useful for eleven years, and everyone in this story would like to
know what changed.

IF THEY ARE INNOCENT — the true explanation
The bell stopped mid-ring, so she went up, found the door bolted and
immovable, went down two floors for the master key, came back and got in
at 11:54, not 11:58 — she moved the time so that the fifteen minutes
she spent in that room alone with a dead man would not have to be
explained. She spent them looking for her file. The safe was already
standing open and already empty when she got there and she went through it
anyway, twice, with her hands shaking, which is where the prints came
from. Then she telephoned the Sparrow from his own telephone with him
sitting four feet away, because she could not think of a single person in
the Family she would rather have found this than her.

IF THEY ARE THE CULPRIT — the tell
The telephone call. She tells the Sparrow, in the first conversation, that
she called before she called anybody in the Family — and she called
from the office. Push on that one line and the fifteen minutes come apart,
and what she was doing in them is the case. She is also the only person in
the building who knew the bell had rung and stopped, which is to say the
only person downstairs who knew somebody was up there with him.
