THE BOOK

A quarto ledger bound in oxblood leather, about two hundred pages, roughly
half of them used. The Cardinal kept it in the safe behind the Modigliani
and wrote in it himself in a small upright hand, in a shorthand of his own
that four people can partly read.

It is not an accounts book. The Family's money is Arturo Fein's problem and
lives in ordinary ledgers in ordinary places. This is the other book: who
owes what and who told what to whom. Nineteen years of it.

WHAT IS IN IT, THAT MATTERS TONIGHT
- What a Calder Bay police lieutenant has taken since 1930, by year.
- Which Bellandi talked, in 1946, and what it bought them.
- The real name of a woman who has been running the Vermillion's casino
  floor for eleven years on sixty dollars' worth of paper.
- Every dollar of a client escrow account that exists only on paper.
- A page about the Sparrow. What is on it depends on the Sparrow. It is
  there, and more than one person in this building has read it.

WHO WANTS IT
Everyone. That is not a figure of speech: there is no person in this story
who is not better off holding it, and by the second day several of them have
decided the Sparrow already has it, which is a dangerous thing for several
people to believe at once.

WHERE IT IS
Whoever opened that safe took it. That person is not necessarily the person
who fired the shot — the safe was opened after the Cardinal was already
dead, and the two events are hours apart in intention if not in time. Some
of the people who took it will try to sell it. Some will try to burn it. One
of them only ever wanted the four pages that were about them and does not
much care about the rest.

Finding out who opened the safe feels exactly like solving the murder and is
not solving the murder. That is the trap this case is built around, and the
Sparrow should be allowed to fall into it.
