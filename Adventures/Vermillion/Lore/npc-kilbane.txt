DET. LT. ROY KILBANE

Calder Bay PD, 44, on the Carrow pad since he made sergeant nineteen years
ago. At the party in a tuxedo he did not pay for, drinking a tab that is
not his. He is the reason nobody is calling this in. He is also a
genuinely good detective who stopped being one in 1934 for reasons he can
still recite from memory, and he investigated the Bellandi uncles in 1947.

THE LIE
He says he was at the long bar from half eleven to midnight. The barman
says he went off at twenty to and came back at five to, and the barman is
quite certain, because he had to find the bottle Kilbane drinks.

THE GAP
11:40 to 11:55.

THE TRACE
A police-issue evidence envelope in the office bin. Empty, unsealed, and
with the reference line filled in — in his handwriting — for a case
number that is his own.

THE MOTIVE
Eleven days ago, in this building, the Cardinal told him the federal
attorney wanted somebody and that a lieutenant was a reasonable price for
six months of quiet.

IF THEY ARE INNOCENT — the true explanation
He went up to beg. He brought his own file — nineteen years of it, the
thing that would end him — in that envelope, to trade for not being the
one handed over. He got as far as the sixth-floor landing, stood there for
several minutes working himself up to knocking, could not do it, took the
papers out, put the envelope in the office landing bin because he did not
want to be carrying it, and went back down to the bar and drank. He will
confess to almost anything before he will admit that he stood outside a
door and could not knock. He is also, for those several minutes, standing
on the only landing that matters, which makes him a witness to whoever
came out — except that nobody did, while he was there, which is worth
far more than he realises.

IF THEY ARE THE CULPRIT — the tell
The time. On the first night, before the medical examiner has looked at
anything and while Josephine Marsh's estimate is still an estimate she
hedges out loud, Roy Kilbane says eleven forty-seven. Flat. To the minute.
He covers it with thirty years of police instinct and a story about a case
in 1938, and it is a good cover, and he is the only person in Calder Bay
who could get it exactly right that early.
