THE COMMISSION

A privateering commission — a letter of marque — is a piece of paper from
a colonial governor authorising its holder to take enemy shipping. With
one, a captain is an instrument of policy. Without one, the same voyage
is piracy and ends on a rope.

It is the only difference between the two, and it is made of paper.

The captain of the Recompense holds one that was not honestly obtained.
It names things that are not true and was signed by a hand that had no
business signing it. It has never been tested by anyone whose opinion
carries weight.

A commission is not permanent. It is issued against a particular war and
lapses when that war does, which means a captain can go to bed lawful and
wake up a pirate without having done anything at all. Governors also
revoke them, and are not obliged to explain.

The paper matters most in the places where it is read carefully: a King's
ship stopping a stranger at sea, a customs officer in a Spanish port, an
assizes. Everywhere else it is a formality nobody asks for.

Vance knows the commission is false. Vance has known since the first day,
has never said so ashore, and both of them know the other knows.
