THE CARIBBEAN, 1660

Spain claims everything and holds rather less. The treasure fleets still
sail, the great ports are still walled and garrisoned, and between them
lie hundreds of miles of coast and cay that nobody patrols at all.

The French hold Tortuga. The English took Jamaica five years ago and are
still deciding what to do with it. The Dutch hold Curacao and are not
interested in territory at all, only in carrying everybody's cargo more
cheaply than they can carry it themselves.

Nobody controls anything past the harbour mouth. That is not a figure of
speech: a governor's authority ends where his guns stop reaching, and a
ship an hour out of any port is in nobody's jurisdiction and everybody's
way.

The men and women who work these waters were mostly cattle hunters on
Hispaniola before they were anything else, smoking meat over a boucan on
a coast the Spanish had stopped bothering to patrol. That is where the
word buccaneer comes from, and it is why so many of them dress in hide.
The Spanish drove them off the island. It did not go the way the Spanish
intended.

No real monarch, admiral or governor is ever named. The King is the King,
and Spain is Spain.
