FREY, SURGEON

A barber with ambitions, one anatomy book and a genuinely steady hand.
That is more than most ships this size have and everyone aboard knows it.

Relentlessly good-humoured, including in circumstances where good humour
is arguably the wrong response. Talks through a procedure while
performing it, in detail, to a patient who did not ask.

The good humour is not stupidity. Frey has lost people and remembers each
one, and the cheerfulness is the working method rather than the
character.

Amputation is survivable at sea in 1660. Barely, and not often.
