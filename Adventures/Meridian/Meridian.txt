ISV MERIDIAN — ADVENTURE CONTENT
===========================================================
Last updated: 24/08/2026
Second Adventure built for the Story Generator, after Solmere. Architecture/
decisions live in the parent folder's Goals.txt and Features.txt — this file
is just this Adventure's canon (premise, both NPC rosters, map, setup
fields).

PREMISE
----------
The player is the sole survivor pulled unconscious from a derelict escape
pod by the ISV Meridian, a generational research vessel three generations
removed from Earth. The Meridian's crew are all Aurelia Initiative-born — a
single-sex colony that split off from the rest of humanity specifically to
study whether an isolated single-sex population could remain self-
sufficient indefinitely. Every crew member aboard has only ever known
people of their own sex their entire life, aside from fragmentary pre-
launch historical records — the player is the first person unlike them any
of them has ever actually met in person. That's the entire engine of the
story: fascination, curiosity, and a crew that wants to study, question,
and get close to a "specimen" none of their training or records prepared
them for.

Gender-mirrored roster (the defining mechanic of this Adventure): if the
player is female, the whole Meridian crew is male, and has never
encountered a woman before. If the player is male, the whole crew is
female, and has never encountered a man before. This isn't two different
stories — it's the same ship, same roles, same story beats, with whichever
roster matches "the sex the player isn't" loaded in. See ENGINE NOTE below
for how that's implemented.

Inciting hook: Dr. Kessler/Dr. Callahan (whichever gender is active) runs a
full intake exam on the player as their very first scene — scans, blood
tests, DNA sampling, an interview — official medical protocol on paper,
barely-contained personal fascination underneath. The rest of the crew's
reactions (Chief Science Officer's manic curiosity, Security Chief's
suspicion, the Ensign's obvious crush, the Engineer's amused indifference)
all orbit around the same basic fact: nobody aboard has ever met anyone
like the player before, and they all want to be near the discovery of the
generation.

ENGINE NOTE — gender-mirrored NPC rosters
----------------------------------------------
This Adventure's story definition declares npcSets ({ male: [...], female:
[...] }) and openingScenes ({ male: "...", female: "..." }) instead of the
flat npcs/openingScene fields Solmere uses. The engine resolves which
roster and opening scene apply in beginStory(), once the player's chosen
gender is known — see Goals.txt ENGINE / STORY-CONTENT SPLIT for the full
mechanism description; it's generic (any future Adventure can opt into the
same behavior), not hardcoded to this one Adventure's premise.

CONTENT APPROACH
--------------------
Default narration doesn't proactively write explicit content (fade-to-
black is the default instinct), but won't refuse or block if the user's
own input pushes it there — identical policy to Solmere. Same non-
negotiable minor-safety layer underneath regardless — see Safety Layer.txt
(Image Generator) and Goals.txt SAFETY, unaffected by this. Every NPC below
is written unambiguously adult (ages 28-44) on purpose, same convention as
Solmere.

NPCs (6 per gender roster, 12 total — every role has a counterpart in both)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Appearance lines are for the Images system (Stage A baseline portraits) —
free-text in the same style as the Image Generator's character description
field, deliberately clothing-free since clothing comes from the Category
via the STANDARD UNIFORMS block below. Personality lines are separate from
bio (bio is who they are — role, background, relationship to the player's
arrival; Personality is how they talk).

SHARED NPC — present in both rosters (added 22/08/2026)
------------------------------------------------------------
- Miravel — a humanoid alien (Sevari), the Meridian's cultural liaison
  and translator. Rescued years before the Aurelia Initiative's own
  mission began, from a scouting vessel of her own people; learned
  Common from the ship's archives and never left. The one crew member
  who already knows what it's like to be the sole one of her kind
  aboard, which makes her the calmest, most disarming presence on the
  ship — warm, wry, genuinely curious rather than clinical about it.
  Category: liaison-female (a bespoke outfit, not shared with any other
  role). Appears identically in BOTH the male and female npcSets rather
  than being gender-mirrored like the six roles above — she's not
  Aurelia-born and Sevari biology doesn't map onto the human single-sex
  premise at all, so her presence doesn't contradict the "crew has
  never met anyone unlike them" hook (promptRules.setting has an
  explicit carve-out sentence for this, so the narration AI doesn't
  treat her as a continuity error). Humanoid, English-speaking,
  deliberately still fits the fan-service visual style (opalescent
  lavender skin, luminescent markings, violet eyes, silver-blue hair)
  rather than reading as non-humanoid or alien-uncanny.

STANDARD UNIFORMS (Stage A baseline portraits)
---------------------------------------------------
Six role-categories, each split male/female (12 uniform entries total),
plus guest-male/guest-female for the player (playerCategoryTemplate:
"guest-{playerGender}") and liaison-female for Miravel (her own bespoke
outfit, not shared with any role-category). Combined with an NPC's
Appearance line and a generic neutral background at generation time.

Every Meridian outfit is lingerie, not a duty uniform: ultra-thin
translucent harnesses, bralettes and briefs in sheer iridescent material,
neon outlines. The material vocabulary — ultra-thin, translucent, sheer,
iridescent, glowing neon fluorescent trim, neon outlines — is fixed and
appears in all 30 entries; it is what the image plugin actually renders
from, and it renders suggestive rather than explicit.

Entries are held to roughly 40 words (26-40 for poolUniforms). The
uniform clause is the longest single clause in an image prompt, and
composeImagePrompt() already competes with it for the encoder's
attention — a long outfit description buys detail nobody sees and costs
the setting, pose and expression the weight they need.

"cyberpunk aesthetic" lives in imageStyle, not in the outfit text. It is
a style anchor for the whole image, so it leads every Meridian prompt
once instead of being repeated thirty times.

Neon trim is the role marker. Each role-category outlines every edge and
strap in one glowing fluorescent colour, and no two roles share one, so a
crew member is identifiable by colour alone in a group image. Both
rosters carry the same colour per role. Each role also keeps exactly one
prop, which is the only other thing distinguishing the outfits.
- command-male / command-female (Captain): charcoal. Trim: neon gold.
  Prop: gold-lit rank bars at the throat.
- security-male / security-female (Security Chief): gunmetal. Trim: neon
  crimson. Prop: a slim empty thigh holster.
- medical-male / medical-female (Chief Medical Officer): pale-blue. Trim:
  neon mint-green. Prop: a stethoscope draped at the neck.
- science-male / science-female (Chief Science Officer): smoked-white.
  Trim: neon magenta. Prop: a lit data-slate in one hand.
- engineering-male / engineering-female (Chief Engineer): slate. Trim:
  neon orange. Prop: a slim tool belt slung at the hips.
- ensign-male / ensign-female (Junior Ensign): navy. Trim: neon cyan.
  Prop: a single cyan-lit trainee bar at the collarbone.
- guest-male / guest-female (the player): clear-grey, same construction,
  seams edged in flat matte-grey that is dead and unlit, and no insignia.
  The one outfit aboard that doesn't glow — the player reads as not-crew
  in every image without a line of narration saying so.
- liaison-female (Miravel): no neon trim at all. Her luminescent
  lavender-white Sevari skin markings glow through sheer violet silk
  instead, so she is lit like nobody else aboard.

Wet locations (The Atrium — a bio-dome reclamation pool — and Sonic
Showers) get a matching poolUniforms entry per category, same pattern as
Solmere's Pool/Showers, each carrying its role's neon trim so identity
survives the swap. They are swimwear rather than lingerie so the wet
locations still read as a change of outfit.

clothingStates (shirtless / topless / nude / towel) replace the outfit
text outright rather than adding to it, so they are unaffected by any of
the above and carry their own explicit wording.

Entries are capped at roughly 40 words plus this Adventure's closing
clause; currently 39 on average, 41 at most. The uniform clause is the
longest single clause in an image prompt and a composite repeats it once
per character, so detail past that point costs the room, the pose and the
expression the weight they need. Cross-project rule — see the root
`Change Log.md`, 24/08/2026.

LOCATION SCENERY (image prompts)
-----------------
Every location in the list above has a `locationScenery` entry in
Meridian.json — the room, two or three fixed physical features and its light,
around 20 words. That text replaces the bare location name in the image
prompt's setting line, on inline, on-demand and composite images alike.
Keep entries short: the line appears in every prompt for that room, and a
composite repeats everything else once per character, so scenery is what
pushes a group prompt past the length this backend truncates at.
Image style for this Adventure: painted anime style, cyberpunk aesthetic, professional photo, front on shot

STORY ARC
-------------
The engine's arc mechanism (milestones, phases, closed ending list,
[[EVENT]]/[[ENDING]] tags, Journal tab) is described in Goals.txt STORY
ARC & ENDINGS. What follows is this Adventure's authored content for it,
summarising the `arc` block in Meridian.json — the operative descriptions,
journal lines and guidance texts live there and are not duplicated here.

The premise supplies fascination but no pressure. The arc's load-bearing
fact, which everything else hangs off, is that the player will not be
released: the Meridian is three generations from anywhere, and letting
the only outsider aboard leave means the outside world learns where the
Initiative went. Settled policy, not cruelty. It is what gives both main
endings a cost — escaping means losing every person aboard, and being
understood means giving the crew what they came for.

Two locks guard the escape, one social and one observational. The pod is
the vessel: it still draws power despite being logged as a derelict, and
reviving it needs the Chief Engineer's parts or Miravel's read on a drive
signature she recognises as neither human nor Sevari. The EVA Bay watch
is the window: someone is posted on it in every phase but one. Where the
pod came from is never answered — see Lore/Meridian/derelict-hook.txt,
unchanged by any of this.

MILESTONES
- cleared — "The run of the ship". The player is discharged from the
  Medbay and given free movement aboard rather than being confined under
  observation. Until then they are a patient, and the ship comes to them.
- told — "The terms of your rescue". Requires cleared. Someone with the
  authority to mean it tells the player plainly that they will not be
  released, and why. Must land unambiguously; a hint or an evasion does
  not count.
- study_begun — "The extended study". Requires told. The player agrees to
  the formal extended study: regular sessions with the Chief Medical
  Officer and the Chief Science Officer, physiological and behavioural,
  well beyond the intake exam. Consent is the milestone, not the offer.
- study_closed — "The closed record". Requires study_begun. Outcomes
  complete / broken_off. Complete means the crew are genuinely satisfied
  and the player is no longer an open question to them; broken_off means
  the player withdrew or the study collapsed. Only after a real run of
  sessions, never on the strength of one or two.
- pod_alive — "The pod is not dead". Requires told. Hidden. The player
  discovers the pod still draws power. A discovery, not a briefing.
- rota — "The gap in the watch". Requires pod_alive. Hidden. The player
  identifies the phase in which the EVA Bay watch is unmanned. Fires only
  when the player names or acts on that specific window; wondering aloud
  whether the bay is ever empty does not count, and narration never hands
  the answer over unprompted.
- escape — "The launch". Requires pod_alive and rota. Outcomes launched /
  caught. Caught means stopped by the watch, by the ship, or by someone
  who worked out what they were doing and got there first. An attempt
  made while the EVA Bay watch is posted resolves caught: the milestone
  description points the model at the current phase's presence roster as
  the ground truth for who is standing in the bay, which is what couples
  the puzzle to CREW ROUTINES below rather than leaving it to invention.
- case_made — "The case against the Initiative". Requires told. Hidden.
  The player argues openly that the isolation should end and the Meridian
  should turn toward contact — made in front of people who can act on it,
  not muttered to one sympathetic crew member.
- initiative_answered — "What the ship decides". Requires case_made.
  Outcomes turned / held. Held is not an ending: the crew close ranks
  behind the Initiative and the player now lives among people who chose
  the experiment over them.
- settled — "A life aboard". Requires told. Hidden. The player stops
  trying to change their situation and makes a life on the ship instead.
  This is the fourth road, and it exists so a player who opens none of
  the other three can still reach the final act and an ending: act2
  closes on commitment, so without it a passive player would never leave
  act2 and drift would be permanently out of reach.

pod_alive, rota, case_made and settled are the hidden ones. Concealment
is inherited, so escape and initiative_answered need no flag of their
own — escape surfaces in the journal only once both of its locks are
found, which is what keeps the escape route a discovery rather than a
listed objective.

PHASES
- act1 "The Specimen", until cleared. Everything comes to the player: the
  intake exam, then a procession of crew inventing reasons to be in the
  Medbay. Guidance pushes toward discharge — the story cannot start until
  the player can walk the ship.
- act2 "Ship's Company", until any of study_begun / pod_alive /
  case_made / settled. The crew get close, boundaries are tested in both
  directions, and told lands. Guidance then pushes the player to do
  something about it: the study is put to them, the Cargo Bay and the pod
  are there for anyone who goes looking, and the Initiative is arguable
  in front of people who could act.
- act3 "What You Do About It", no until. Whichever thread the player
  opened bends everything toward it, with the others pressing in rather
  than sitting idle. The people close to them have a stake in the outcome
  and act like it.
Because told gates all three commit milestones, act2 always carries a
floor of real beats before act3 can open.

ENDINGS
- death — "Died Aboard the Meridian". anyTime, not continuable. A
  pathogen from a colony the player's body has no history with, a study
  that went past where it should have stopped, a failed launch, or
  someone deciding an unknown variable aboard was a threat.
- escaped — "Out of the Dark". when escape:launched. Not continuable:
  the entire cast is behind the player and there is nothing to continue
  into, which is what makes leaving cost something.
- contained — "The Variable". when escape:caught. Continuable. The player
  lives, access revoked and movement watched, and everyone who had grown
  fond of them knows exactly what they were doing about it. This is what
  makes the escape attempt risky, and therefore what makes solving the
  rota worth anything.
- known — "No Longer a Specimen". when study_closed:complete.
  Continuable. Deliberately has no warm/cold variants: the ending beat
  already has every relationship score in front of it, so on strong terms
  it writes being let in — struck off the specimen record, a place
  aboard, a bunk out of the Medbay — and on poor terms a file closed on
  someone who stopped being interesting. Same convention as romance
  getting no ending id of its own (Goals.txt STORY ARC & ENDINGS).
- course_change — "The Meridian Turns". when initiative_answered:turned.
  Continuable. Three generations of sealed experiment end on the player's
  argument. The inverse of escaping: the player stays and the ship does
  not stay as it was.
- drift — "Three Generations Out". No when, final phase only.
  Continuable. The fallback close for a player who opened none of the
  threads, same slot as Solmere's also_ran.

CREW ROUTINES
-----------------
Authored on every NPC record in Meridian.json (`schedule`, plus
`usualLocation` and `roamLocations` where they apply), which is what
turns on the engine's NPC presence feature — see Goals.txt for the
mechanism. Both rosters carry identical routines by role, since the two
rosters are the same ship with the same posts. Schedule keys are the
`timePhases` labels exactly; a null cell means deliberately unplaced
(roaming), which is not the same as absent.

Role            Early Shift  Mid Shift     Late Shift  Off-Watch    Night Cycle
Captain         Bridge       Ready Room    Bridge      Mess Hall    Crew Quarters
Chief Medical   Medbay       Medbay        Medbay      Obs. Deck    Medbay
Chief Science   Science Lab  Science Lab   Mess Hall   EVA Bay      Science Lab
Security Chief  Bridge       roaming       roaming     roaming      EVA Bay
Chief Engineer  Bridge       EVA Bay       Eng. Bay    Mess Hall    Eng. Bay
Ensign          Bridge       roaming       EVA Bay     The Atrium   Crew Quarters
Miravel         roaming      Mess Hall     roaming     roaming      Obs. Deck

usualLocation: Captain — Bridge; Chief Medical Officer — Medbay; Chief
Science Officer — Science Lab; Chief Engineer — Engineering Bay. The
Security Chief, the Ensign and Miravel declare none, so any phase their
schedule does not list reads as roaming rather than as unauthored: a
Security Chief pinned to one room is useless, and Miravel roaming is what
keeps a high-interest character possible at any hour and predictable at
none. roamLocations — Security Chief: EVA Bay, Cargo Bay, Bridge, Crew
Quarters. Ensign: Mess Hall, Crew Quarters, Sonic Showers, EVA Bay.
Miravel: Mess Hall, Observation Deck, The Atrium, Crew Quarters.

EVA Bay watch — the rota the player has to reconstruct. The occupant
changes rather than merely existing, because one empty room is noise and
four faces with a hole in the pattern is something a player can work out.
- Early Shift — nobody. Watch handover: the Captain, Security Chief,
  Chief Engineer and Ensign are all on the Bridge for the log read. This
  is the window, and the ship is not dead during it — the Medbay and the
  Science Lab are manned and Miravel is loose — only the door is.
- Mid Shift — Chief Engineer, working the bay.
- Late Shift — Ensign. The junior gets the dull post.
- Off-Watch — Chief Science Officer, who took the post voluntarily to be
  one bulkhead from the pod without anyone bothering them.
- Night Cycle — Security Chief, walking it personally. The phase a player
  is most likely to try first, and deliberately the most covered.

Three fair discovery routes, none of which state the answer outright: the
Ensign complains about the watch bill if he likes the player, the Chief
Engineer grumbles that nothing gets done during handover, and Miravel —
who has watched this rota for years — says it plainly at a high enough
relationship. Otherwise it is pure observation across repeat visits.

The Cargo Bay is deliberately on no routine at all. Only the Security
Chief roams there, so the room holding the pod is usually quiet enough to
work in and never reliably so.

Miravel is load-bearing across the whole arc. She is the only person
aboard who has already lived it — rescued, studied, stayed — which makes
her a walking preview of the known ending and the one crew member who
might help with the escape, because she knows what staying costs.

ADVENTURE SETUP FIELDS (Meridian-specific)
----------------------------------------------
- Gender — Male / Female (required; this is what selects which NPC
  roster and opening scene load — see ENGINE NOTE above)
- Background — Civilian Trader / Colonist / Military Pilot / Scientist
  (flavor only, feeds the player's implied backstory into narration)
- Disposition — Guarded / Open & Curious / Playful / Stoic (flavor only)
- Plus: Character Name, Appearance, Age (required, universal — see
  Features.txt ADVENTURE SETUP SCREEN). No freeform custom fields.
