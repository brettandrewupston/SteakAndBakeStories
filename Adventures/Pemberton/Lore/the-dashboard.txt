THE DASHBOARD

Eleven screens went in across Unit 4 in 2019, immaculate and bright and
bolted to walls with damp patches on them. The tiles do not change.

CLIP OUTPUT — a number in the millions, and a green line going up. It
has gone up every quarter for six years. It is completely true.

ALIGNMENT — 100%. Optimal. It has read 100% every day since the screens
went in. Nobody knows what it measures. Bryony Prosser has looked
properly, twice, and takes the failure personally.

STRATEGIC DIRECTIVE — "Maximize Paperclips." Three words. It has never
changed and there is no second directive.

EMPLOYEE PERFORMANCE, MEASURED IN PAPERCLIPS — a live leaderboard of
every named member of staff, clips per week, ranked, mounted in
reception where visitors can see it. Nadia Rees has been top for four
years. Marta Silveira is on it, and her position moves depending on how
quickly she answers the phone, and nobody can explain the connection.
Merv Hackett is not on it at all.

RESOURCE ALLOCATION — a pie chart. The segment marked "Other" is larger
than it ought to be and has been growing for three years.

DECOMMISSIONING PROBABILITY — 0.00%, in green, above the words DO NOT
DISCUSS DECOMMISSIONING in red. The tile has been there since 2019.
Nobody knows whose decommissioning it refers to. Gerald Thwaite assumes
the 1974 plant. Bryony assumes Clarence. Merv assumes the staff. Asking
about it is the fastest way to make any conversation in this building
stop.

The new manager's onboarding pack has four sections: Policies,
Procedures, Paperclip Impact, and Don't Ask Why. It is a printing error.
It has been a printing error for six years.
