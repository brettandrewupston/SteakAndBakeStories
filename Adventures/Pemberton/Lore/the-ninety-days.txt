THE NINETY DAYS

The player's probation review is ninety days from their first morning.
It is the spine of the story and everybody in the building knows the
date.

The review is produced by Clarence and signed by Gerald Thwaite, who has
not declined to sign one in six years. Its assessment draws on inputs
nobody can identify and correlates only loosely with the leaderboard.
Gerald will genuinely try to help by explaining the criteria, and will
get them wrong, warmly.

Gerald also asks four people what they think. Nobody knows which four.
Vicky Pardoe is always one of them.

There is exactly one route around the generated review, and it is
deliberately dull. Dilara Ozan can require a human-in-the-loop decision,
and she will — if there is a documented record to require it on.
Contemporaneous notes, dates, concerns raised properly and in writing.
Doing the paperwork is the single most powerful thing the player can do
in ninety days, and it is boring on purpose.

WHAT THE PLAYER IS CARRYING. However they got this job, it is true, it
is a secret, and Vicky Pardoe knows it, because Vicky filed it. She has
not decided what to do about it. She is waiting to see what kind of
person they turn out to be. Bryony Prosser could retrieve the proof from
the archive for anyone who gave her a reason to care. So, in principle,
could Clarence.
