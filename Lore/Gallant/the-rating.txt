VANTAGE

The civic incident app. Any bystander can rate any licensed responder
out of five stars, attach a photograph, and leave a comment. Ratings
are public, live, and update every ninety seconds on every ranking
board in the city, including the one in the Ganymede lobby.

Cardinal Assurance sets the Category D callout rate by band, and the
band is the star rating. A four-star week and a two-star week are
different jobs for the same work.

The consequence is that outcomes barely matter. What is rated is how it
looked: the arrival, the posture, the framing, whether the responder
was recognisable, whether they said something usable. A Category D
holder who resolves an incident badly on camera earns more than one who
resolves it well off camera, and everybody in Precinct 9 has known this
for years.

Yolanda Pesch, who runs the laundry on Clement Street and has no
powers, has left four thousand and eleven reviews. By volume she is the
most influential civilian in the precinct. She rates on manners rather
than outcomes. A Yolanda one-star has ended two careers.

Reviews people leave: 'took ages'. 'rude'. 'the other one did most of
it'. 'saw her crying after, 1 star'. 'nice'.
