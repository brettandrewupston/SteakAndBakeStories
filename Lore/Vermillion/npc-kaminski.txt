KAMINSKI

Ruth "Ruthie" Kaminski if the Sparrow is a man, Reuben "Rube" Kaminski if
the Sparrow is a woman — the same person either way, and the Sparrow's
partner of six years. Army medic through Italy and France. Drives, carries
what the Sparrow will not, throws the punches, and keeps the notebook,
which means Kaminski is the only physical record of this case and
everybody in the building has worked that out by the second day.
Kaminski was in the Vermillion all night, on the floor, watching doors,
which puts Kaminski in the timeline exactly like everybody else, and
nothing in this story is going to pretend otherwise.

THE LIE
Kaminski says the north doors, half eleven to midnight, unbroken. The
doorman says he was relieved at 11:52 and that nobody was on those doors
before that.

THE GAP
11:33 to 11:52.

THE TRACE
A page torn out of the notebook. The stub is still in the binding and the
impression of what was written on it is still on the page underneath.

THE MOTIVE
There is a page in the Cardinal's book about the Sparrow. A case the
Sparrow got wrong, years ago, and what it cost somebody. Kaminski has
known it was in there for a year, has never mentioned it, and has been
asked twice by two different people what it would be worth to make it
disappear.

IF THEY ARE INNOCENT — the true explanation
Kaminski went up to ask for the page. Not to steal it, not to trade for
it — to ask, which is exactly as naive as it sounds and exactly what
Kaminski would do. Never got past the lift lobby, waited on the landing
below for a while working out a better sentence, gave up and went to take
the north doors. The torn page is not about the case at all. It is what
Kaminski had written about the Sparrow, over some months, in a notebook
the Sparrow reads, and it was torn out at about a quarter to twelve
because after six years there is a limit to how long a person will leave a
thing like that lying where it might be read. It is in a coat pocket. It
has not been thrown away.

IF THEY ARE THE CULPRIT — the tell
The impression on the page underneath. Hold it to a lamp and it is not a
sentence — it is eight digits in two groups of four, in Kaminski's
handwriting, and they are the combination of a safe on the sixth floor.
