MARCO "THE DEACON" VASILE

Enforcer, 40. Nineteen years with the Carrows, no arrests, because nothing
he does leaves anybody willing to say so. He is standing outside the
office door when the Sparrow arrives and he has been told nobody goes in.
He cannot be moved by force by anybody in this building. He speaks about
the Cardinal in the present tense and has not noticed he is doing it.

THE LIE
He says he was on the sixth-floor stair from half past eleven. He was on
it from 11:52. Before that he was not in the building at all, and he will
not say where he was, and he will not lie about it either — asked
directly, he simply stops speaking, which everybody in the Family reads as
a confession and which is the closest thing he has to a defence.

THE GAP
11:30 to 11:52. Twenty-two minutes, the largest gap of anybody, entirely
unaccounted for, and he does not appear to care.

THE TRACE
Gun oil on his hands and a cleaning kit in his coat pocket. He cleaned a
weapon that night. He will confirm that without being asked twice.

THE MOTIVE
In November the Cardinal ordered him to kill his cousin Petru over eleven
hundred dollars and a rumour. He did it. He has been at five o'clock mass
every morning since and everybody in the Family knows why.

IF THEY ARE INNOCENT — the true explanation
Father Nunzio from St Silas came to the alley service door at half past
eleven because Vasile asked him to, and Vasile made his confession
standing in the snow between the bins — about Petru, in detail, for
twenty minutes. It is a perfect alibi and neither man will ever give it.
Vasile will not, because a confession is a confession. Nunzio will not,
because a confession is a confession. Reaching it means getting the priest
to say a thing he believes he will be damned for saying, or getting Vasile
to release him from it, and the second is easier than the first and still
very hard. The gun oil is the Cardinal's own pistol: he cleaned it at
eight o'clock, in the office, at the Cardinal's request, and put it back
in the drawer himself.

IF THEY ARE THE CULPRIT — the tell
The tense. He talks about the Cardinal in the present tense throughout,
consistently, without exception — the man who cannot lie has not
accepted it — and once, early, before the body has been announced to
the room, he uses the past tense about him and corrects himself. Nobody
notices except the Sparrow, and only if the Sparrow is listening to how he
speaks rather than to what he says.
