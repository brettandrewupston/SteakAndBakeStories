ARTURO FEIN

Consigliere, 46. Lawyer, immaculate, and the Family's entire memory. He
drafted every will the Cardinal ever signed, including the unexecuted one
on the desk. He is the only man on the floor in plain black and he is the
most expensive thing in the building.

THE LIE
He says he never left the private booths. The booth waiter says he left
twice, and can say what he was carrying the second time, which was
nothing on the way up and nothing on the way back.

THE GAP
11:38 to 11:52.

THE TRACE
The new will itself, in his handwriting, unexecuted, on the desk. He says
he delivered it on the afternoon of the thirtieth. The ink has not been
dry for a day and any competent examiner will say so.

THE MOTIVE
Three years of client escrow routed through an account that exists on
paper only. The Cardinal's new auditor found the shape of it in December
and Arturo has known for six weeks exactly how long he had.

IF THEY ARE INNOCENT — the true explanation
The second trip was to the count room, by the freight stair, carrying
forty thousand dollars in cash under his coat to put it back before the
audit. He was making himself whole. The count-room clerks will confirm the
money appeared and will not confirm who brought it, because they did not
see a face — they heard the racks move, which is a noise they have
learned not to turn around for. Saying any of this means admitting both
the theft and that he knows the freight stair is open, and he would rather
be tried for murder than struck off, which is not a joke and is the single
most accurate thing anybody could say about him.

IF THEY ARE THE CULPRIT — the tell
The will is dated the thirty-first. He says he delivered it on the
thirtieth. He does not correct the discrepancy, and he does not correct it
again when it is repeated back to him. Arturo Fein corrects everything. He
has corrected a man's grammar during his own arraignment. A factual error
left standing in front of him is the most unnatural thing in this case.
