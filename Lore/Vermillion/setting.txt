SETTING — NOBODY CALLS THE POLICE

Calder Bay, 31 December 1949, and the six storeys of the Vermillion.

The war made the Carrow Family rich and the peace made them respectable, and
they spent both on this building. A neon marquee legible from the water. A
supper club with a twenty-girl chorus line and a headliner whose name is on
the sign. A casino floor under a mirrored ceiling. An indoor pool called the
Grotto, lit turquoise from below, open all night. A count room in the
basement where four men do nothing but weigh money. And on the sixth floor,
one office with a view of the whole bay.

At 11:47pm, with eight hundred people downstairs screaming at the ceiling
and the band covering the countdown, Don Ottavio Carrow — the Cardinal —
was shot once in that office. The door was bolted from the inside.

The player is the Sparrow: the detective the Families call when they cannot
call police. Paid in cash, protected by reputation, and famously unarmed.
Josephine Marsh found the body and telephoned the Sparrow before she
telephoned one single person in the Family.

Three days until the funeral. Nobody is calling the police, because half the
police are downstairs in rented tuxedos and one of them is a suspect.

WHAT IS TRUE IN EVERY VERSION OF THIS STORY

- Time of death 11:47pm. The countdown covered the shot. Nobody in the
  building heard it.
- The weapon is the Cardinal's own pistol, out of his own desk drawer, wiped
  and left on the blotter. Nobody carried a gun up to that room. This murder
  was decided by somebody already standing in it, which is why the killer is
  not calm afterwards and has not had time to be clever.
- The safe was opened after he was already dead, by somebody who knew the
  combination. This is the hinge of the whole case: being placed in that
  office is not proof of murder, and two separate people went in there.
- The book is gone.
- There were two glasses on the desk. One poured and untouched. He was
  expecting somebody, and he poured for them, which means it was somebody he
  was not afraid of.
