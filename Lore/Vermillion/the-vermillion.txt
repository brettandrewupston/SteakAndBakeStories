THE VERMILLION

Six storeys on Marchant Street, opened 1947, and the most expensive thing in
the state that is not a bridge.

GROUND — THE SUPPER CLUB FLOOR. Two hundred tables around a horseshoe stage,
a bandstand for eighteen, and a ceiling of mirrored panels that throws the
whole room back at itself. Delphine Rosetti headlines two sets a night with
twenty girls behind her. The gold doors face Marchant Street; the service
doors face the alley.

THE CASINO FLOOR. Through the arch behind the bandstand. Roulette, craps,
blackjack and a private baccarat room. Josephine Marsh runs it and hires
every dealer on it herself.

THE CHORUS ROOM. Backstage, one long mirror, twenty stools, and the only
room in the building where anybody says what they think.

THE GALLEY. Kitchens, and the service dumbwaiter that runs from here to the
office pantry on six. It was put in so the Cardinal could eat without coming
down. It is small.

THE COUNT ROOM. Basement. Four clerks, two scales, a steel door and a
schedule. Behind the racks at the back is the old freight stair, which runs
the full height of the building and was welded shut in 1946.

THE GROTTO. An indoor pool on the second floor, tiled deep blue, lit
turquoise from underneath, open all night. Half the deals in this building
get made in it.

THE PRIVATE BOOTHS. Mezzanine level. Curtained, telephone in each, and where
the Cardinal did business when he did it downstairs.

SIX — THE CARDINAL'S OFFICE. A desk, a bar, a wall of glass over the bay, a
safe behind a Modigliani that everybody in the Family has watched him open,
and a private powder room off the landing. One door. A heavy brass bolt on
the inside of it, which the Cardinal threw himself every night of his life,
because a man who has had two attempts on him does not sit with his back to
an unlocked door.

THE ALLEY. Service doors, bins, the staff smoking spot, and the only six
square metres of Calder Bay the Carrows have never bothered to buy. Anything
honest that gets said in this building gets said out here.
