DELPHINE "DEL" ROSETTI

The Vermillion's headliner, 31, and the name on the marquee. Six years the
Cardinal's mistress and six New Year's Eve promises that he would leave
Vivienne, the most recent one made ten days ago. Her contract with the
club is worth exactly what the Carrows decide it is worth, and no other
room in the state will book her while the Family is annoyed.

THE LIE
She says she was in the chorus room from the end of her set until the
countdown. Nineteen of the twenty girls say she went out. The twentieth is
her dresser and says nothing at all, loudly.

THE GAP
11:44 to 11:55.

THE TRACE
A single white ostrich feather on the sixth-floor landing carpet, from her
headdress. One glove from the set she wore in the second half is also
missing and she has not mentioned it.

THE MOTIVE
The seventh promise. And twice in the week before Christmas a federal man
bought her a drink at the Adelphi Hotel bar, and she reported neither
occasion to anybody in this building.

IF THEY ARE INNOCENT — the true explanation
She went up to the office to end it — to tell him she was going, and
that she had somewhere to go and somebody who would make sure she got
there. She got as far as the landing and stopped, because she could hear
two voices through the door and one of them was not one she expected. She
went back down and cried in the ladies' powder room off the mezzanine for
six minutes, which is where the glove is, in the bin, wet. Saying any of
it means saying the federal man out loud in a building owned by the Carrow
Family. She heard two voices. She does not know whose. She is the single
most valuable witness in the case and the hardest to get anything out of.

IF THEY ARE THE CULPRIT — the tell
She describes the office door as bolted. Nobody outside a very short list
knew the bolt was thrown before Josephine Marsh opened that door, and she
is not on it. She says it once, in passing, while talking about something
else entirely, and she does not notice she has said it.
