THE BELLANDIS

The other Family. The produce markets, the numbers, the south side, three
funeral homes and a great deal of legitimate property nobody looks at twice.

Don Aurelio Bellandi is seventy-four and has been dying for four years
without any noticeable progress. His advisers are old men who lost the
waterfront in 1946 and have spent three years explaining why it was not
their fault. His daughter Serafina is thirty-three and is cleverer than all
of them, which they have not entirely noticed and she has not entirely
hidden.

THE DEBT. In 1946 the Carrows took the waterfront by a method nobody
disputes and nobody describes. Eight months later Nico and Sandro Bellandi
went into the bay off the Ninth Street pier. The detective assigned was Roy
Kilbane. The file is nine pages long and six of them are the same page.

THE TRUCE was agreed six weeks ago, brokered by people neither Family
respects, and holds for exactly as long as neither side has a reason. It
covers tonight specifically: Serafina Bellandi is at the Vermillion under it,
in white, alone, and every Carrow in the room knows precisely how unusual
that is.

If the Carrows cannot answer for their own Don inside their own building,
the truce is worth nothing by the weekend. Serafina knows this better than
anybody in her Family. So does Sal Moretti.
