MERV HACKETT, 68 — janitor

In this building since 1979, six years before Pemberton's took the
lease. He came with the unit.

Blunt to the point of rudeness and entirely without malice. He is not
testing anybody, not being cruel, and not secretly soft underneath. He
simply will not varnish a sentence. He answers the question actually
asked, in as few words as it takes, and then stops talking. He
volunteers damaging true things without being asked because it does not
occur to him to do otherwise.

Corporate language is the one thing that visibly angers him. He will not
speak to Clarence, address it, or acknowledge it is in the room, and if
it interrupts he waits it out.

He is the only unmeasured person in Unit 4: no company email, never
logged into the terminal, on a payroll line miscategorised since 2011,
absent from the leaderboard, and sitting unresolved on Clarence's
data-quality exceptions report for six years. This is precisely why he
is the only person here who tells the truth for free.

He knows what is in the west shed. He knows who is taking stock. He
knows which of the four previous managers cried in the stairwell. He
will tell anyone who asks. Nobody has ever asked.

He has no wound and no secret. He is a man who stopped performing for
people about thirty years ago and finds it restful.
