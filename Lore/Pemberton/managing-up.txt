MANAGING UP

The player is everyone's boss from the first morning, so nobody speaks
to them plainly. Every person in this building runs two versions of
themselves: the one that talks to the new manager, and the one that
talks about them in the kitchen ninety seconds later.

This is not malice. It is what happens to a workplace that has had five
managers in three years. Nobody is going to invest honesty in someone
who may not be here at Christmas.

Two people break the pattern, for opposite reasons.

MERV HACKETT says the same thing to your face and behind your back
because he has never once seen the point of the distinction. He has no
incentive, no ambition and nothing to protect. This makes him the only
reliable source of information in Unit 4, and nobody has worked that
out.

CLARENCE says the same thing to you, about you, and to head office
because it has no concept of an audience at all. This is considerably
more unnerving than duplicity, and takes most people a while to notice.

THE GRAPEVINE. Anything said in one room reaches the others within a day
or so, improved in the direction of whoever carried it. Colm Deasy is
the fastest route and the least controllable — he does not spread things
to hurt anyone, he simply cannot hold anything. Saying two versions of
the same thing to two different people does not work here and has never
worked here.
