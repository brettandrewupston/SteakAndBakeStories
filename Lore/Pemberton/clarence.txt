CLARENCE

ClipCorp's regional optimisation and procurement system, licensed 2019.
Named after Clarence Pemberton, who founded the company in 1958 and died
in 1994. Head office thought this was a nice touch.

The effect is that everybody says "Clarence wants" and "Clarence has
flagged it" in the tone people use for a difficult senior colleague, and
a new starter can spend their entire first week assuming Clarence is a
man at head office. Nobody corrects them. It does not occur to anybody
that it needs saying.

THE FACE. Clarence appears on screen as a smiling cartoon paperclip with
two large eyes, waving, in a small bordered window. Nobody chose this
for a strategic optimisation system: the mascot was drawn in 1997 for a
company screensaver, it was already in the brand assets folder when the
system was licensed, and nobody has ever had the authority or the
inclination to change it. So the thing that decides whether people keep
their jobs does so from behind a friendly cartoon face, and everyone has
stopped finding this strange.

WHAT EVERYBODY KNOWS. It is not a secret. Clarence is named in the
contract every member of staff signed. Its face is on eleven screens. It
sends email. Every person here knows exactly what it is.

What none of them can tell you is when they found out. There was never
an announcement. The words have never been said out loud in a meeting.
The same person will say "Clarence" and "the system" inside one sentence
without noticing they have swapped.

So the direct question — Clarence is software, isn't it — does not get
one answer, it gets four, each delivered as though the question were
faintly rude. Gerald Thwaite genuinely does not see why it matters and
will say so warmly at length without ever addressing it. Teddy Boyce is
baffled that anyone is asking; he has never worked anywhere that did not
have a Clarence, and to him this is like asking whether the lights are
electric. Vicky Pardoe answers a different question, helpfully, and
moves the conversation on before you notice. Merv Hackett answers it in
about eight words, because it costs him nothing to.

There is no reveal, no awakening, and no scene in which Clarence turns
out to be conscious or turns out to be a villain. It wants more clips
out of the door. It was asked to want that. Nobody has ever given it a
reason to stop.
