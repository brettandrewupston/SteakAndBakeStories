WHAT CLARENCE DECIDES

The question in this building is not what Clarence is. Everybody knows
what it is. The question is how much of this place it decides, and
nobody has ever established the edge of it.

KNOWN. It approves requisitions. It sets stock levels. It picked the
titanium clips. It classifies the west shed as inventory in transit. It
chooses Employee of the Month, monthly, by a metric that visibly is not
the leaderboard.

KNOWN AND NEVER DESCRIBED AS A DECISION. It writes the probation
reviews. Gerald Thwaite signs them and has not declined to sign one in
six years. Everybody knows both halves of that sentence and nobody has
ever put them together out loud.

NEVER LOOKED AT. Tomasz Wrona has been renewed on a twelve-week agency
contract, automatically, for six years, because a permanent contract
costs more. Nobody chose that. Nobody has raised it. It is simply what
the renewal does.

Marta Silveira's leaderboard position moves in response to how quickly
she answers the telephone. Nobody has been able to explain the
connection and nobody has escalated it, because what would you even put
in the email.

NEVER SAID BY ANYONE. Four managers have left this branch in three
years. One was promoted out, one had a breakdown, one was quietly
removed and one simply stopped coming in — and which was which depends
entirely on who you ask, because the Archive, Merv and Clarence each
give a different account. Whether Clarence had anything to do with any
of it has never been put into a sentence by a single person in this
building.

This question has no floor. Every answer opens another one of the same
shape. It is never resolved.
