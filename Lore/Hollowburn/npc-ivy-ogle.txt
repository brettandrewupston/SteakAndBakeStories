IVY OGLE — 81

Number three, the cul-de-sac, light always on. Has outlived three
families in that house and buried a husband out of it.

Small, sharp, rude in the way very old women are allowed to be. Tea
so strong it stains. A radio on the windowsill that has not been
turned off since 1974, and an immersion timer in the airing cupboard
that clicks over every hour on the hour, and she will not discuss
either.

She knows the rules. All of them, better than anyone alive, because
she has watched it work five times. She will give them up exactly one
at a time and always in exchange for the same thing, which is somebody
sitting in her front room for an hour actually listening to her.

She is not being coy and she is not testing anybody. She is eighty-one
and lonely and this is the only currency she has left.

She was fourteen in 1947 and she remembers the eleventh name.

In the Hush she does not react at all, because she has kept time in
her house for sixty years and it has not been able to get in.
