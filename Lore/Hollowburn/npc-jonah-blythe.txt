THE BLYTHE CHILDREN

Peter and Evelyn have two. The elder is twenty-one and has not slept
properly in four months. The younger is nineteen. The player is the
elder one; the younger one is the NPC, and which is which depends on
the gender chosen at the start.

  Female player — the player is MARA, 21. Her brother JONAH is 19.
  Male player   — the player is JONAH, 21. His sister MARA is 19.

Only one of the two is ever a character in a given story. Never refer
to the player in the third person by the sibling's name, and never put
both siblings in a scene as separate people.

THE YOUNGER ONE, whichever they are

Tall enough to fill a doorway, dark brown hair, glasses they look at
people over the top of. Drives a car with a dent in it that is not
their fault and which they will tell you about.

Funny, loud, physically affectionate in the way siblings are —
headlocks, a childhood nickname nobody outside the house is allowed to
use. Genuinely, immovably loyal. Also completely unequipped for any of
this, and everyone including them knows it.

They believe their sibling long before they believe in the thing, and
that distinction is the most important one in the story. They do not
need to accept that there is something on the landing. They only need
to accept that their brother or sister is not lying, and sit up with
them, and that alone stops it dead.

Which is what makes them the target. Each of them sleeps under a sound
that carries through the wall — Jonah snores through a closed door,
Mara falls asleep with a podcast running and never turns it off. Either
way the house can hear that they are under, and either way the sound
stopping is how you know it has started on them.

Mara's podcast is not a habit. It is a ward she does not know she has.
Sound that keeps time is the one thing the Marra cannot hold a room
against, which is why Ivy Ogle has slept with the radio on for sixty
years and never told anybody why. Nobody in the house has worked this
out. If the player works it out, they have found the only piece of real
protection anyone in Hollowburn has stumbled into by accident — and they
have also worked out exactly what happens the night it gets switched off.

They have been arguing with Barry Kelso about the car, and anger is a
handle.
