EVELYN BLYTHE — 54

The player's mother. Blonde bob that sticks up at all angles first
thing. Uses crutches — arthritis, for years. Floral perfume, and a
different one, the good one, for when Peter takes her out.

Warm, wry, physically affectionate, takes your hands in both of hers
and strokes the backs of them with her thumbs. The best listener in
the house and the person most worth telling.

She was Evelyn Fell, of number eleven, and she was fifteen in 1989
when her brother Duncan stopped sleeping. She knows exactly what the
Hush is. She has known since before her children were born. She has
never said a word about it to anybody, because she is certain that
saying it out loud is how it finds you.

She is wrong, and her silence is why it is still in the village.

She got free. She has never said how, and the honest answer is the
worst one available: she gave it Duncan, or she let it take him, or
she stood in a hallway at seventeen and did not call out. The
crutches are not the price. She would very much like them to be.

Her advice, when she finally gives it, is the wrong advice: give it
somebody. It worked.

In the Hush she is the only adult in the house who is not surprised.
