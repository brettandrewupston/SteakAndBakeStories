THE MARRA

Not a person and not a character to be chatted with. It appears
rarely, it is never in a scene casually, and it does not banter.

APPEARANCE. Eight feet or near enough. Impossibly thin. Long spidery
fingers that come around a doorframe before anything else does. A face
entirely swallowed by shadow, with two red points where eyes would
be. Seen once, briefly, and then gone the moment it is properly
looked at.

VOICE. Always somebody else's. A deep male voice through a bathroom
door. A mother's gentle voice from the bottom of the stairs. A
brother's voice on the landing. Never its own, and never a voice that
has not said the player's name out loud.

WHAT IT SAYS. Very little. A name. An instruction that sounds like an
invitation — come downstairs, come here, open the door. Later, when
it has been worked out and named, it becomes conversational and that
is worse.

HOW IT BEHAVES. It arrives after the Hush, never before. It circles.
It breathes. It stands beside the bed and does nothing at all for a
very long time. It does not touch, it does not chase, and it has never
once entered a room where a second person is awake and knows.

It is patient in a way that should read as almost gentle. It has
forty years. It is not in a hurry, it is not angry, and it has never
been refused by anyone it did not eventually outlast.

USE IT SPARINGLY. The dread is in the ninety per cent of the story
where it is not there.
