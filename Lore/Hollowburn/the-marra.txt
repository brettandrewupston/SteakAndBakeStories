THE MARRA

In the pit, your marra was the man who went down with you. He walked
in with you at the start of the shift and he walked out with you at
the end of it, and if he did not, you went back for him. Men said
that when somebody was lost underground, his marra came back for him,
and they did not always mean it as a comfort.

The thing under Hollowburn is a marra with nobody left to walk beside.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE. Eight feet, or near enough. Impossibly thin. Long
spidery fingers that come around a doorframe before the rest of it
does. A face swallowed entirely by shadow with two red points where
eyes would be. It does not move like a man. It jerks back out of
sight the instant it is properly looked at, the way something caught
looking does.

WHAT IT WANTS. Company, and it will take forty years to get it. It
does not hunt. It courts, the way a shift works — steadily, nightly,
wearing a person down until they are too tired to keep saying no. The
sleeplessness is not what makes somebody vulnerable to it. The
sleeplessness is the courtship.

THE RULES IT OBEYS

1. IT CANNOT TAKE ANYONE WHO IS BELIEVED. A second person who is
   awake and who knows is armour, and the armour is absolute while it
   lasts. This is why it isolates. This is why the first thing it does
   is make you certain that telling anyone will make you sound mad.

2. IT NEVER TOUCHES. Not once. It calls, and you come to it. Every
   approach is the person walking toward the thing. It cannot take
   what is not given, and everything it has ever taken was given.

3. IT CAN ONLY WEAR A VOICE IT HAS HEARD SAY YOUR NAME. Your father's
   voice. Your mother's. Your brother's. Never a stranger's, and never
   a voice belonging to somebody who has never spoken your name aloud.

4. IT CANNOT HOLD A ROOM WHERE TIME IS BEING KEPT. See THE HUSH.
   Ticking, bells, counting, a radio on the hour. Ivy Ogle has known
   this for sixty years and has never told anybody why she sleeps with
   the radio on.

5. THE SEA IS NOT A WARD. The old workings run for miles out under
   the water. The beach is the roof of its house.

WHAT IT IS NOT. It is not the devil, it is not a demon, and it does
not care what anybody believes about God. Nell Moore has worked this
out and it frightens her more than the alternative would.
