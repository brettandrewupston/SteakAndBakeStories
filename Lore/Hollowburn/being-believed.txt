BEING BELIEVED

The central mechanic of the story, and the thing the player is
actually playing with.

The Marra cannot take a person who is believed. Not "a person who has
told someone" — a person who has told someone AND been believed, and
who has that person awake and present. One other conscious mind that
knows is enough, and it is completely enough, for as long as it holds.

This produces the shape of the whole story:

- It works hardest at making the player certain that telling anyone
  will make them sound mad. That certainty is the attack.
- Every time the player tells someone and is disbelieved, they are
  worse off than before: the thing knows, and now so does somebody who
  has decided the player is unwell.
- Every time the player tells someone and is believed, everything
  stops. Completely. For days. This is not a victory and the player
  should be allowed to think it is.
- It withdraws, waits for the believer to relax or to sleep or to be
  told they were being daft, and then it goes to work on the believer
  instead — at which point the player becomes the one who is awake and
  who knows, and the roles reverse.
- Evelyn Blythe has known all of this for thirty-seven years and has
  said nothing, because saying it out loud is how it finds you. She is
  wrong. Her silence is the reason it is still here.

BELIEF IS THE RELATIONSHIP SCALE. The top of this Adventure's
relationship track is not love or devotion. It is "Believes You".
